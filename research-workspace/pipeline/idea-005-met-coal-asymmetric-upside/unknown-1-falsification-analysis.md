## Findings

### Finding 1: HCC trades at ~9–10.5x forward EV/EBITDA — far above the thesis's 4x claim and the 5.5x kill threshold

- **Source**: company filings, analyst estimates, financial data aggregators
- **Source URL/Reference**: [StockAnalysis HCC Statistics](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/hcc/statistics/), [Moody's via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/moodys-revises-warrior-met-coals-outlook-to-positive-on-blue-creek-progress-93CH-4391738), [BMO Capital via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/warrior-met-coal-stock-price-target-raised-to-55-from-50-at-bmo-capital-93CH-4176802)
- **Credibility**: medium — forward EBITDA estimates are derived from multiple sources rather than a single consensus figure, introducing estimation error; however, all triangulation methods converge on the same conclusion
- **Content**: HCC's enterprise value stands at approximately **$4.55–5.23B** (Yahoo Finance reported $4.55B on Jan 2, 2026; StockAnalysis reported $5.23B in mid-January as the stock rallied further). Consensus FY2026 EBITDA estimates range widely: **Moody's projects ~$300M** (using conservative $180/MT met coal), **BMO Capital's target implies ~$500M**, and **triangulation from consensus EPS of $5.41** on 52.57M shares yields ~$284M net income, which at historical EBITDA/NI ratios of ~1.7x produces approximately **$480–510M EBITDA**. WallStreetZen reports FY2026 average net income of $306M from 2 analysts, implying ~$460–550M EBITDA. Even using the most generous EBITDA figure of $550M against the lower EV of $4.55B, the forward EV/EBITDA is **~8.3x**. Using the mid-case ($500M EBITDA, $5.0B EV), it is **~10x**. This is roughly **2x the 5.5x kill threshold** and **2.5x the thesis's 4x claim**.
- **Bearing on thesis**: This is the single most damaging finding. The thesis claims HCC trades at 4x EV/EBITDA, but it now trades at 8–10x on consensus FY2026 estimates. The stock has already re-rated well beyond the alleged entry point. **Directly supports the kill condition.**

### Finding 9: EAF steelmaking capacity is surging — structural met coal demand threat accelerating

- **Source**: industry reports
- **Source URL/Reference**: [Carbon Brief Analysis](https://www.carbonbrief.org), [IEEFA Met Coal Projections](https://ieefa.org)
- **Credibility**: high — respected energy and climate research organizations
- **Content**: **93% of new steelmaking capacity announced in 2024 uses electric arc furnace (EAF) technology**, up from 33% in 2022. EAF's share of the global steel fleet is projected to rise from **24% in 2020 to 36% by 2030**. Global met coal trade is forecast to decline from **349Mt (2023) to 333Mt by 2029**, with China's met coal imports expected to fall **27% by 2029** as the country builds DRI plants at commercial scale. The EIA projects modest growth in U.S. met coal exports (95 to 101 MMst by 2027), but this is against the backdrop of a structurally shrinking addressable market. The World Bank projected a further **7% decline in coal prices in 2026**.
- **Bearing on thesis**: The thesis's core premise — that "global BF-BOF steel demand sustains met coal volumes through 2027" — faces an accelerating structural headwind. While BF-BOF won't disappear by 2027, the trajectory is clearly negative, which undermines the sustainability of cash flows that the 50%+ upside re-rating thesis depends upon. Markets may be correctly pricing in terminal value risk rather than irrationally discounting these stocks.

## Kill Condition Assessment

**Kill condition from thesis**:
> - HCC or AMR currently trading above 5.5x forward EV/EBITDA on consensus 2026 estimates
> - Both stocks up >40% from pre-2025 steel rally levels with sell-side upgrades already widespread
> - Insider selling at either company in the last 90 days exceeding $5M aggregate

**Evidence FOR kill condition being triggered**:
- **HCC forward EV/EBITDA is approximately 8–10.5x** on consensus FY2026E EBITDA of ~$450–550M against an EV of ~$4.55–5.23B — **decisively above the 5.5x threshold**
- **HCC stock is up 65–90%** from January 2025 levels — **far exceeding the 40% threshold**
- **HCC insider selling totals $12.6M** in the past 90 days, **2.5x the $5M threshold**, with the CEO liquidating 25% of his holdings
- Both HCC and AMR trade **above consensus analyst price targets**, indicating the re-rating has already overshot sell-side expectations
- **HCC received multiple sell-side upgrades** in the past 6 months, including a key UBS upgrade from Neutral to Buy (Jan 29, 2026)
- AMR's forward EV/EBITDA is ~5.3–5.6x — at the borderline of 5.5x, not at 4x as the thesis claims
- AMR's Q4 2025 massive earnings miss could push consensus estimates lower, increasing forward EV/EBITDA further

**Evidence AGAINST kill condition being triggered**:
- **AMR stock is essentially flat** from January 2025 (~$200 → ~$200) — does **not** meet the >40% threshold
- **AMR insider buying is massively bullish**: $22M in purchases vs $1.5M in sales — well below the $5M selling threshold
- **AMR analyst coverage remains extremely thin** (2–3 active analysts), supporting the "uncrowded/capital exile" thesis
- AMR's forward EV/EBITDA of ~5.3–5.6x is borderline and could be below 5.5x if met coal prices recover above consensus
- Forward EBITDA consensus estimates are derived (not directly observed) and carry significant estimation uncertainty
- The wide dispersion in HCC EPS estimates ($2.21–$7.31 consensus range, B. Riley at $10.59) means the forward EBITDA range is wide — at the very high end ($940M per Fintel), HCC would be at ~5.6x

**Assessment**: **PARTIALLY TRIGGERED**

The kill condition is **clearly triggered for HCC** across all three criteria (EV/EBITDA well above 5.5x, stock up >40%, insider selling >$5M). For AMR, the kill condition is **not triggered** on stock performance or insider selling (which is actually deeply bullish), but the valuation is at the **borderline** of the 5.5x threshold (~5.3–5.6x), not at the thesis's claimed 4x.

The thesis as originally stated — referring to both "HCC, AMR" as a paired trade at "4x EV/EBITDA" with "50%+ upside" — is significantly weakened because HCC has already re-rated, and AMR's valuation is meaningfully above 4x. However, a narrower thesis focused exclusively on AMR retains some validity.

**Confidence in assessment**: **medium-high** — The HCC conclusion is robust across multiple estimation methods. The AMR conclusion is more uncertain because forward EBITDA estimates are derived rather than directly observed, and the stock's 50%+ decline-then-recovery within 2025 makes the "starting point" for measuring re-rating ambiguous. The Q4 2025 earnings miss adds further uncertainty to forward estimates.

## Caveats and Limitations

- **Forward EBITDA consensus figures were not directly available** from free financial data sources for either company. All forward EV/EBITDA calculations rely on triangulated estimates derived from EPS consensus, revenue forecasts, and assumed margin/D&A relationships. Actual Bloomberg or FactSet consensus EBITDA figures could differ materially.
- **Analyst estimate dispersion is unusually wide** for both companies, reflecting extreme sensitivity to met coal prices. HCC FY2026 EPS ranges from $2.21 to $10.59; AMR ranges from $6.23 to $23.86. This makes any point estimate of forward EV/EBITDA inherently uncertain.
- **Stock prices are volatile and data points span a multi-week window.** HCC traded between $90 and $105 in January–February 2026; AMR swung from $253 to $200 following Q4 results. The precise EV/EBITDA at any given moment depends on which price and which estimate you use.
- **AMR's Q4 preliminary results (Jan 30, 2026) likely triggered analyst estimate revisions** that may not yet be reflected in consensus databases. If FY2026 EBITDA estimates are revised downward, AMR's forward EV/EBITDA would increase, potentially pushing it more clearly above 5.5x.
- **10b5-1 trading plans complicate the insider selling signal** for HCC — the CEO's sales were scheduled in November 2023, before the 2025 rally. However, the magnitude ($10M+) and failure to cancel or amend the plan despite favorable market conditions remains informative.
- **The thesis's original timing and price levels are unknown.** If it was formulated when HCC was at $38–45 (mid-2025) and AMR was at $97 (June 2025), the 4x EV/EBITDA claim may have been approximately accurate at that time.

## Summary

The falsification research reveals a **bifurcated picture**: the thesis has largely failed for HCC but retains partial validity for AMR. HCC has already undergone a dramatic re-rating, driven primarily by the Blue Creek mine coming online 8 months early. At ~$90–100/share, HCC trades at approximately 8–10.5x forward EV/EBITDA on consensus FY2026 estimates — roughly double the 5.5x kill condition and 2.5x the thesis's claimed 4x multiple. The re-rating is confirmed by the stock's 65–90% surge since January 2025, multiple sell-side upgrades (including a key UBS upgrade to Buy), and — most damningly — the CEO liquidating 25% of his personal holdings for $10M+ in January 2026. The "easy money" in HCC is clearly gone.

AMR presents a more ambiguous case. Its stock is essentially flat year-over-year despite massive intra-year volatility (a 50% crash to $97 followed by a 105% rally to $254, then a pullback to ~$200 after a brutal Q4 earnings miss). Forward EV/EBITDA sits at approximately 5.3–5.6x — above the thesis's 4x but borderline relative to the 5.5x kill condition. Critically, AMR's insider activity is the strongest bullish signal in the entire analysis: Director Kenneth Courtis invested ~$30M of personal capital in the stock over 6 months, while the 10% owner added $7.3M. Analyst coverage remains razor-thin at 2–3 active sell-side analysts, and the stock still meets the "uncrowded/capital exile" criteria. However, the Q4 2025 earnings miss and CEO's warning of "another challenging year" in 2026 suggest forward estimates may need downward revision, which would push the EV/EBITDA multiple higher.

The strongest counterargument to the thesis is not valuation alone but **structural demand erosion**: 93% of new global steelmaking capacity is EAF-based, met coal trade volumes are projected to decline, and China's imports could fall 27% by 2029. This undermines the thesis's core assumption that BF-BOF demand "sustains" met coal volumes through 2027. Markets may be rationally pricing in terminal value risk rather than irrationally punishing these stocks due to ESG capital exile. For HCC specifically, the re-rating was driven by a company-specific catalyst (Blue Creek) rather than the sector-wide thesis the investment case relies upon — a distinction that matters because it means the "rising tide lifts all boats" argument is weaker than it appears.
