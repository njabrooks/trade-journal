### Unknown 3: HCC/AMR Cost Competitiveness vs. Australian Producers

#### Falsification Track

**Research Date**: 2026-02-06
**Objective**: Find evidence that U.S. met coal pure-plays are at a structural cost disadvantage vs. Australian competitors

**Kill Condition Assessment**:
> - HCC/AMR cash cost per ton >$120 FOB mine (making them uncompetitive below $200/ton HCC benchmark)
> - U.S. met coal export volumes declining QoQ despite rising global demand (share loss)
> - Freight rates from Hampton Roads/Baltimore to Asia >$40/ton above Newcastle/Hay Point to Asia
> - HCC or AMR reporting declining export volumes or shifting to lower-margin domestic sales

**Status**: PARTIALLY TRIGGERED (AMR triggers on cost/volumes/shift; HCC defensible on mine-mouth cost but inferior on delivered basis)

**Findings**:

1. **AMR true FOB port costs $122-145/st (mine cost + $25-35 rail to Hampton Roads)**
   - Source: AMR Q4 2024, Q1 2025 filings (PRNewswire)
   - Credibility: 0.85 (mine cost from filings; rail cost estimated)
   - Bearing: Exceeds $120 kill threshold on all-in FOB port basis. Four consecutive quarterly losses confirm marginality.

2. **HCC costs declining to ~$101/st FOB port with Blue Creek — below kill threshold**
   - Source: Warrior Met Coal Q4/FY2024, Q1 2025 filings
   - Credibility: 0.90
   - Bearing: Below $120 and improving. But FY2024 was $125.29/st, and $138/mt is still above Stanmore's $89/mt.

3. **Stanmore Resources at $89/mt FOB (ex-royalties), ~$109/mt all-in — $20-30/mt below HCC**
   - Source: Stanmore Annual Report 2024
   - Credibility: 0.90 (audited financials)
   - Bearing: Structural cost advantage. Record 13.8Mt production. Eagle Downs development optionality.

4. **BHP BMA costs elevated at $127.50/mt but declining; Q4 FY25 hit $110/mt**
   - Source: BHP FY2025 results
   - Credibility: 0.90
   - Bearing: BHP is higher cost than HCC at mine-mouth, but produces PLV-grade coal vs HCC's 86-89% realization. Quality-adjusted basis favors BHP.

5. **U.S. met coal exports collapsed 48% YoY in Aug 2025 to 8-year low**
   - Source: EIA, S&P Global, The Coal Hub
   - Credibility: 0.95 (government data)
   - Bearing: Triggers kill condition 2. U.S. share of global trade falling from ~15% toward 10-12%. Mongolia overtook U.S. as #2 exporter.

6. **Combined freight+rail disadvantage to Asia reaches $35-55/mt**
   - Source: IEA Coal 2025, Coal Age analysis
   - Credibility: 0.75 (ocean freight estimated from distance proportionality; rail from industry estimates)
   - Bearing: Partially triggers kill condition 3. Ocean alone is $15-25/mt; combined with rail differential ($18-28/mt) exceeds $40/mt.

7. **CRU: Two-thirds of U.S. met coal unprofitable at $170/t PLV**
   - Source: CRU Group via Steel Market Update
   - Credibility: 0.90 (leading met coal cost-curve consultancy)
   - Bearing: U.S. industry positioned in third/fourth quartiles globally. Only HCC Alabama operations are first/second quartile. U.S. Senate passed emergency tax credits acknowledging distress.

8. **HCC realizes only 86-89% of PLV benchmark — no quality premium**
   - Source: Warrior Met Coal 10-K, SEC filings
   - Credibility: 0.95 (company disclosure)
   - Bearing: Directly contradicts conviction-increase condition 3 ($15-25/t quality premium). AMR worse: 68% High Vol A/B at 40-45% discount to PLV. Average AMR export realization ~$119-127/t.

9. **China tariffs (34%+) closed the largest met coal import market to U.S. producers**
   - Source: EIA, CRS, S&P Global
   - Credibility: 0.95 (government sources)
   - Bearing: China accounted for 76% of H1 2025 export decline. Australian producers face no barriers. U.S. coal forced into markets where it is less competitive.

10. **Whitehaven Queensland met coal assets at ~$55-57/mt (ex-royalties)**
    - Source: Whitehaven FY25 results
    - Credibility: 0.90
    - Bearing: World-class cost positioning with Japanese steelmaker partnerships (Nippon Steel 20%, JFE 10% of Blackwater). Structurally superior to U.S. pure-plays.

**Caveats**: U.S.-to-Asia coal freight rates estimated (not a standard benchmark route); FOB mine vs FOB port reporting differences create comparison difficulty; Queensland royalty structure adds $15-20/mt at higher prices; PLV benchmark recently rallied to ~$233/mt due to flooding/force majeures — may not sustain; tariff situation is dynamic.

## Evidence Synthesis

### Summary (by Theme)

1. **HCC Has Already Re-Rated — Thesis Timing Has Failed**
   The thesis claims HCC trades at 4x EV/EBITDA. It currently trades at 8-10.5x. The stock is up 65-90% since January 2025, driven primarily by the Blue Creek mine completion (company-specific catalyst, not sector ESG de-rating). The CEO has liquidated 25% of his personal holdings. Sell-side coverage is warming rapidly (UBS upgrade, multiple PT raises). The entry point described in the thesis no longer exists for HCC.
   - Supported by: U1-F1 (EV/EBITDA), U1-F2 (stock performance), U1-F3 (insider selling), U1-F6 (above targets), U1-F7 (upgrades), U1-F10 (Blue Creek)
   - Strength: **strong** — multiple independent data points converge

2. **AMR Is Marginally Positioned, Not ESG-Discounted**
   AMR's apparent cheapness (~5.3-5.6x forward) reflects genuine business distress, not irrational ESG discounting. True FOB port costs ($122-145/st) exceed the kill threshold. Product quality is inferior (68% High Vol A/B at 40-45% discount to PLV). Four consecutive quarterly losses with shipment guidance cut twice. CRU data places most U.S. met coal in third/fourth cost quartile. However, this is contradicted by extraordinary insider buying ($22M+), suggesting at least some sophisticated investors see a turnaround.
   - Supported by: U3-F1 (AMR costs), U1-F8 (AMR losses), U3-F7 (CRU cost curve), U3-F8 (PLV discount)
   - Contradicted by: U1-F5 (AMR insider buying — $22M)
   - Strength: **strong** for marginality, but insider signal creates material uncertainty

3. **U.S. Structural Export Disadvantage to Asia — Wrong Equity Expression**
   The $35-55/mt combined freight+rail disadvantage to Asia, no quality premium (11-14% discount to PLV), China tariffs closing the largest market, and export volumes at 8-year lows collectively indicate U.S. met coal equities are the wrong expression for global met coal demand. Australian alternatives (Stanmore at $89/mt, Whitehaven QLD at $55-57/mt) offer structurally superior cost positions, proximity to growth markets, and direct Japanese steelmaker partnerships.
   - Supported by: U3-F5 (export collapse), U3-F6 (freight), U3-F8 (no quality premium), U3-F9 (China tariffs), U3-F3 (Stanmore costs), U3-F10 (Whitehaven)
   - Strength: **strong** — the freight/quality/market access disadvantage is structural, not cyclical

4. **The "ESG Capital Exile" Narrative Is Partly Misattributed**
   The thesis posits that valuations are depressed due to ESG-driven capital exile. Evidence suggests the valuation gap is partially a rational freight-and-quality discount (U.S. producers genuinely deliver less value per ton to Asian buyers) and partially a reflection of business distress (AMR is losing money). HCC's re-rating was company-specific (Blue Creek), not evidence of ESG capital flowing back to the sector. The narrative oversimplifies the reasons for low multiples.
   - Supported by: U1-F10 (Blue Creek drove HCC re-rating), U3-F6 (freight discount), U3-F8 (quality discount), U3-F7 (cost curve positioning)
   - Strength: **moderate** — ESG exclusion is real but is only one of several discount drivers

5. **Structural Demand Erosion From EAF Acceleration**
   93% of new steelmaking capacity announced in 2024 is EAF. EAF share projected to rise from 24% (2020) to 36% (2030). Global met coal trade projected to decline from 349Mt to 333Mt by 2029. China met coal imports may fall 27% by 2029. This undermines the thesis's core claim that BF-BOF demand "sustains" met coal volumes through 2027. Note: Unknown 2 (China steel) was not researched, so this theme relies on secondary evidence.
   - Supported by: U1-F9 (EAF data, Carbon Brief, IEEFA)
   - Strength: **moderate** — directionally clear but sourced from only one research track; Unknown 2 not investigated

## Gate Assessment

**Thesis Status**: modify

**Rationale**:

The thesis as stated — "Global BF-BOF steel demand sustains met coal volumes through 2027 while ESG-driven capital exile keeps U.S. pure-plays (HCC, AMR) at 4x EV/EBITDA — creating 50%+ upside to re-rating" — is largely falsified on its equity expression but potentially valid on its core demand insight.

**What's wrong:**
- HCC has already re-rated to 8-10x (kill condition triggered across all criteria)
- AMR is operationally distressed, not merely ESG-discounted (4 consecutive losses, 2/3 of U.S. met coal unprofitable at $170/t)
- The "4x EV/EBITDA" premise is factually incorrect for HCC and borderline for AMR
- U.S. equities face a structural $35-55/mt freight+rail disadvantage to Asia and no quality premium
- The "ESG capital exile" narrative is partly wrong — the discount reflects freight, quality, and operational realities

**What may still be valid:**
- BF-BOF irreplaceability for ~70% of global steel production (untested in this research)
- India's coking coal import growth trajectory (73.5Mt in 2025, +32% YoY)
- Met coal supply constraints from ESG underinvestment in new capacity
- AMR insider buying signal ($22M+) suggests sophisticated investors see value
