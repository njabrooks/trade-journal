# Thesis: Met Coal Equities as Leveraged Steel Demand Expression

## Core Thesis (25 words max)

Global BF-BOF steel demand sustains met coal volumes through 2027 while ESG-driven capital exile keeps U.S. pure-plays (HCC, AMR) at 4x EV/EBITDA — creating 50%+ upside to re-rating.

## Primary Economic Driver

**Global blast furnace utilization rates** — specifically the ratio of BF-BOF steel output to total crude steel production. As long as BF-BOF maintains ~70% share globally and utilization stays above 75%, met coal demand is structurally underpinned. The driver is not "steel demand" generically (EAF steel doesn't help) but BF-BOF steel specifically.

## Value Chain Impact

```
India/Asia infrastructure spend → BF-BOF steel production ↑ → Coking coal demand ↑
                                                                      ↓
                                  Seaborne met coal price ↑ ← Supply constrained (ESG underinvestment)
                                                                      ↓
                                  U.S. Appalachian met coal producers → Margin expansion → FCF ↑
                                                                      ↓
                                  Buybacks + dividends at 4x EV/EBITDA → Per-share value acceleration
```

**First-order effects**: Met coal spot and contract prices rise, lifting revenues for pure-play producers.
**Second-order effects**: Elevated FCF at depressed multiples funds aggressive buybacks (AMR) and special dividends, amplifying per-share returns without requiring multiple expansion.
**Third-order effects**: Sustained outperformance forces ESG-excluded funds to reconsider or creates activist pressure to unlock value (spin-offs, take-privates).

## Primary Beneficiaries

- **HCC (Warrior Met Coal)**: 100% met coal, premium HCC grades, Alabama longwall operations. Most direct pass-through from met coal prices. Stock historically leads the met coal benchmark by 1-2 months. Quality positioning = pricing power.
- **AMR (Alpha Metallurgical)**: 100% met coal, largest U.S. producer by volume. Aggressive buyback program creates financial leverage on top of operating leverage. Highest beta to met coal price moves.
- **METC (Ramaco Resources)**: Smaller met coal producer with higher growth rate. Rare earth extraction optionality provides embedded call option. Trades at ~$600M market cap — most convex to re-rating.
- **SXC (SunCoke Energy)**: Coke producer with long-term take-or-pay contracts to Cleveland-Cliffs/US Steel/ArcelorMittal. Domestic steel production rising = mechanical coke demand increase. Lower vol, contracted cash flows.

## Primary Victims

- **EAF-only steelmakers (NUE, STLD)** at the margin: If BF-BOF economics strengthen relative to EAF (lower met coal vs. higher electricity/scrap costs), integrated steelmakers gain share. Not a direct loser, but relative positioning shifts.
- **Green steel / H2-DRI ventures**: Sustained BF-BOF competitiveness delays the economic case for hydrogen-based steelmaking, pushing timelines further and reducing venture/project funding urgency.
- **ESG-mandated funds**: Continued coal equity outperformance creates tracking error for ESG-constrained indices, potentially pressuring the mandate model.

## Failure Modes

### 1. China Property Collapse Triggers Global Steel Recession [structural]

**Description**: China produces ~55% of global steel. A severe deepening of the property sector contraction reduces Chinese steel demand by 10%+, crashing global BF-BOF utilization rates. Even with India growing +9%, China's absolute volume dwarfs India's. Seaborne met coal prices collapse below $150/ton, destroying U.S. producer margins.

**Evidence Indicators**:
- Chinese crude steel production declining >5% YoY for 2+ consecutive months (World Steel Association monthly data)
- Australian HCC index dropping below $180/ton
- SLX ETF declining >15% from recent highs
- China rebar futures in sustained contango

### 2. EAF Substitution Accelerates Faster Than Expected [structural]

**Description**: EAF currently holds 30% global share but is growing. If scrap availability improves (especially in India/SE Asia) and electricity costs fall, the BF-BOF share could decline from 70% to 60% within 3-4 years rather than the expected 10-15 year timeline. This structurally shrinks the met coal addressable market, invalidating the demand thesis.

**Evidence Indicators**:
- Global EAF share crossing 35% (up from 30%)
- India announcing major EAF capacity builds (displacing planned BF-BOF capacity)
- Scrap steel export volumes from developed markets increasing >20% YoY
- DRI capacity additions globally exceeding 30 Mt/year

### 3. U.S. Producer Cost Disadvantage vs. Australian Competitors [execution]

**Description**: The thesis is right on met coal demand but wrong on U.S. equities as the expression. Australian producers (BHP Met Coal, Stanmore, Whitehaven) have lower cost structures, proximity to Asian buyers, and better port infrastructure. As met coal demand grows, Australian producers capture the margin while U.S. producers face rising rail/shipping costs and export terminal bottlenecks. The trade works but you're in the wrong equities.

**Evidence Indicators**:
- U.S. met coal export volumes flat or declining despite rising global demand
- Australian met coal export market share increasing
- HCC/AMR reporting rising per-ton cost of production vs. prior quarters
- Freight rate differentials (U.S. East Coast → Asia) widening relative to Australia → Asia

### 4. Steel Cycle Already Priced — SLX +46% YTD 2025 Was the Move [timing]

**Description**: The steel demand improvement may already be reflected in met coal equities. SLX gained 46% in 2025, and met coal producers may have already re-rated. Entering now captures the tail end of the cyclical move rather than the beginning. The thesis was right 12 months ago but the entry point has passed.

**Evidence Indicators**:
- HCC and AMR trading above 6x forward EV/EBITDA (would indicate meaningful re-rating already occurred)
- Met coal spot prices declining from 2025 highs
- Analyst upgrades already widespread for HCC/AMR (consensus catching up)
- Insider selling at HCC or AMR

### 5. Global Recession Destroys Industrial Demand Across the Board [external]

**Description**: A synchronized global recession (triggered by credit event, trade war escalation, or monetary policy error) tanks industrial activity worldwide. Steel demand collapses across all end markets simultaneously. Met coal is a high-beta cyclical commodity — it falls further and faster than diversified commodity plays in a downturn.

**Evidence Indicators**:
- Global manufacturing PMIs below 48 for 3+ consecutive months
- U.S. ISM Manufacturing below 45
- Copper prices declining >20% from highs (leading industrial demand indicator)
- Credit spreads widening >200bps from recent tights
- Yield curve inversion deepening

## Gate Assessment

**Decision**: advance

**Rationale**: Core thesis is specific and falsifiable — it identifies the primary driver (global BF-BOF utilization), the expression (HCC, AMR as pure-plays), the mechanism (inelastic met coal demand + ESG valuation discount), and a time horizon (through 2027). All 5 failure modes have concrete, observable evidence indicators: 2 are structural (China steel recession, EAF acceleration), 1 is execution (wrong equities vs. Australian producers), 1 is timing (already priced from 2025 SLX rally), and 1 is external (global recession). The thesis can be monitored and killed if evidence indicators trigger. Confidence maintained at 0.50 — needs Stage 3 unknown mapping to identify research that could raise conviction.
