# OSATs Can Capture Value in Advanced Packaging: Validation Evidence

Advanced semiconductor packaging presents a **qualified investment opportunity for OSATs** despite TSMC's integration advantages. The evidence supports a nuanced bull case: TSMC's persistent capacity constraints, explicit ecosystem partnership strategy, and OSAT margin accretion in leading-edge services validate that Amkor and ASE can capture meaningful—though not dominant—value through 2028. The thesis is strongest for ASE's margin trajectory and weakest for near-term hyperscaler design wins.

## TSMC capacity constraints remain acute through 2027

TSMC CEO C.C. Wei confirmed in the Q3 2025 earnings call that "our CoWoS capacity is very tight and remains sold out through 2025 and into 2026...we are still working to increase the capacity in 2026." Despite doubling capacity annually since 2023, demand continues to outstrip supply. CoWoS-L and CoWoS-S are **fully booked through 2026** according to TrendForce's December 2025 reporting.

The capacity shortage is creating real customer impacts. Google reportedly **cut its 2026 TPU production target by 25%**—from 4 million to 3-3.2 million units—due to CoWoS packaging limitations, according to Korea Economic Daily and Fubon Research. Morgan Stanley's December 2025 analysis shows NVIDIA has locked in approximately **60% of total CoWoS capacity** through 2027, leaving competitors like AMD, Broadcom, and hyperscaler ASIC programs fighting for the remaining 40%.

TSMC is responding with aggressive expansion. Capacity is projected to grow from **35,000 wafers per month** (end 2024) to **120,000-130,000 WPM** by end 2026—a 3.7x increase. However, Morgan Stanley projects demand of 1 million wafers in 2026 versus approximately 1.1 million in available capacity, suggesting only marginal relief. This structural tightness validates thesis condition #1 and creates the foundation for OSAT opportunity.

## Amkor and ASE have won confirmed advanced packaging design-ins

The OSAT customer win evidence is **moderately strong** but concentrated in specific partnerships rather than broad hyperscaler adoption. NVIDIA's CFO publicly confirmed that NVIDIA is partnering with **Amkor and SPIL for packaging and test in Arizona** as part of NVIDIA's $500B+ U.S. AI infrastructure initiative—causing Amkor shares to rise 8% on the announcement. This is the most significant validation of OSAT positioning in the AI supply chain.

Amkor's Arizona facility represents a **$7 billion investment** across two phases, with Apple confirmed as the "first and largest customer" and NVIDIA as a key partner. The facility will provide the "oS" (on-Substrate) portion of CoWoS packaging for NVIDIA products, with volume production expected in early 2028. TSMC and Amkor signed an MOU in October 2024 to bring **CoWoS and InFO technologies to the U.S.**, with Amkor licensed to use TSMC's patented packaging technologies.

ASE shows promising but less concrete customer traction. On the Q3 2025 earnings call, management stated that "several customers are considering adopting ASE's in-house FOCoS technology, with positive developments expected as early as the second half of 2026." ASE's LEAP (Leading-Edge Advanced Packaging) revenue is growing explosively—from **$250 million in 2023 to a projected $1.6 billion in 2025**, representing 167% year-over-year growth.

**Critical gap**: No hyperscaler ASIC packaging wins (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) have been confirmed for OSATs. All major hyperscaler custom silicon currently uses TSMC CoWoS packaging. This represents the most significant counter-evidence to the thesis.

## TSMC's strategy explicitly preserves space for OSATs

TSMC CEO Wei delivered the most important strategic signal for OSAT bulls during the Q2 2024 earnings call: **"TSMC will only focus on the most advanced back-end technologies, which help our customers in leading-edge products."** This statement, made during the introduction of TSMC's "Foundry 2.0" framework, explicitly carves out space for OSATs in non-leading-edge packaging.

TSMC's capex allocation reinforces this positioning. For 2026's record **$52-56 billion** capex, 70-80% is directed toward advanced process technologies (N2, A16), while only **10-20% goes to advanced packaging and related capabilities**. The company is clearly prioritizing foundry over packaging vertical integration.

The partnership actions match the strategy. TrendForce reports that TSMC "has already begun outsourcing select products—mainly those with simpler RDL (redistribution layer) structures—to these partners." TSMC plans to outsource **240,000-270,000 wafers annually** to OSAT partners in 2026, according to JPMorgan's analysis. Wei confirmed in Q3 2025: "Actually, right now, we are working with one OSAT, a big company and our good partner, and they are going to build their fab in Arizona."

The strategic logic is clear: TSMC uses OSATs to keep customers in the TSMC ecosystem rather than losing them to Samsung. As one analyst noted, this is "a strategy to keep clients within the TSMC ecosystem by subcontracting to OSATs with a neutral nature."

## ASE margins are expanding; Amkor margins remain flat

The margin evidence is **strongly bullish for ASE but mixed for Amkor**, creating a differentiated investment thesis.

**ASE margin expansion is confirmed**. ATM segment gross margins improved from 22.5% in 2024 to **26.8% on a currency-adjusted basis** in Q3 2025—within management's structural target range of 24-30%. CFO Joseph Tung stated explicitly on the Q3 2025 call: "The leading-edge, both packaging and test, I think it's really a margin accretive business for us. At the steady state, LEAP would definitely be both margin as well as return accretive." BofA projects ASE IC ATM gross margins will improve from **~23% in 2025 to 27% in 2026**.

ASE's test segment provides additional margin uplift, operating at approximately **35% gross margins** and growing 2x faster than packaging in 2025. Test is approaching 20% of ATM revenue by Q4 2025, up from 16-18% previously.

**Amkor margins remain stagnant**. Full-year gross margins moved from 14.5% (2023) to 14.8% (2024)—a negligible improvement despite 82% of revenue already coming from "advanced" products. The Q1 2025 margin compressed to 11.9% during Vietnam facility ramps before recovering. Management noted that advanced packaging lines "generate margins above corporate average," but higher material content (54-58% of sales) in advanced packages partially offsets the benefit.

## Competitive dynamics favor a tiered market structure

TSMC maintains dominant positioning in cutting-edge advanced packaging (CoWoS-L, SoIC) where technical barriers are substantial. TSMC is "currently the only foundry capable of mass-producing interposers with line widths and pitches smaller than 1mm," and hybrid bonding requires ISO 1-2 cleanrooms that OSATs lack. However, OSATs are capturing value in three distinct segments.

**First, capacity overflow is structural**. TSMC is outsourcing the WoS (Wafer-on-Substrate) portion while retaining CoW (Chip-on-Wafer) for the highest-margin work. ASE's CoWoS capacity is projected to reach **20,000-25,000 wafers per month by end 2025**—more than tripling current output. Some OSATs (ASE/SPIL) are now handling CoW processes as their technical capabilities improve.

**Second, geographic diversification drives investment**. Amkor's $7 billion Arizona campus and TSMC's own AP7/AP8 expansions reflect customer demand for supply chain resilience. The U.S. CHIPS Act is accelerating this trend, with NIST identifying CoWoS capacity as a "significant chokepoint."

**Third, cost-sensitive applications favor OSATs**. Powertech's PiFO technology is reportedly **30% cheaper than CoWoS-L** with better heat dissipation. For mid-tier AI inference chips, networking products, and automotive applications, OSAT technology is competitive or preferred.

## Thesis validation summary

| Conviction Condition | Evidence Strength | Key Finding |
|---------------------|-------------------|-------------|
| TSMC CoWoS capacity constrained | **Strong** | CEO confirmed sold out through 2026; Google cut TPU targets 25% |
| OSAT design wins from major customers | **Moderate** | NVIDIA-Amkor confirmed; no hyperscaler ASIC wins |
| OSAT gross margins expanding | **Mixed** | ASE strong (+400bp ATM); Amkor flat |
| TSMC ceding mainstream packaging | **Strong** | CEO explicitly stated focus on "most advanced" only; 240K+ wafers/year outsourced |

The investment thesis is validated with differentiation: **ASE presents the stronger value capture opportunity** given margin expansion, LEAP revenue growth (560% through 2026), and test segment leverage. **Amkor's thesis depends more on capacity scale and NVIDIA/Apple partnership execution** by 2028 rather than near-term margin expansion. The primary risk remains TSMC's potential to expand vertical integration as capacity constraints ease post-2027, though current strategic signals suggest this is not the company's intent.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports a **qualified bullish case** for OSAT value capture in advanced packaging. TSMC's structural capacity constraints, explicit ecosystem partnership strategy, and customer diversification demands create real opportunity for Amkor and ASE. However, the value capture is concentrated in overflow capacity, backend assembly, and cost-sensitive applications rather than displacing TSMC in cutting-edge AI chip packaging. For investors, ASE offers the clearest margin expansion story with confirmed LEAP accretion, while Amkor represents a longer-duration bet on U.S. manufacturing localization and NVIDIA/Apple partnership execution by 2028.
