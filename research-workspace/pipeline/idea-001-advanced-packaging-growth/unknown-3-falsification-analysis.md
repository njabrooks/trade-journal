## TSMC CoWoS capacity dominates but doesn't eliminate OSATs

**Evidence Found:** TSMC is executing an aggressive capacity build-out that will capture the vast majority of AI accelerator packaging demand through 2028.

TSMC's CoWoS capacity roadmap shows extraordinary expansion: **35,000-40,000** wafers per month in 2024 scaling to **120,000-130,000** by end of 2026, representing a near-tripling of capacity. Advanced packaging CapEx is growing at **24% CAGR from 2025-2027** according to TrendForce (January 2026). NVIDIA alone has secured **70% of TSMC's CoWoS-L capacity for 2025**, with total AI chip customers locking up over 85% of available capacity.

By 2026, TSMC is projected to handle approximately **~510,000 of NVIDIA's 595,000 total CoWoS wafers** (86%), with similar dominance across AMD, Broadcom/Google, and Amazon. Morgan Stanley research indicates TSMC controls roughly **85% of total CoWoS wafer production** for AI accelerators.

**However**, critical nuance exists: demand continues to outpace supply. TSMC CEO C.C. Wei stated in Q3 2025 earnings that "AI demand actually continues to be very strong, it's more—more stronger than we thought 3 months ago." Google was forced to **cut TPU production targets from 4 million to 3 million units** due to CoWoS constraints (Korea Economic Daily, January 2026). This persistent shortage creates structural overflow to OSATs.

**Kill Condition Assessment:** **PARTIALLY TRIGGERED** — TSMC dominates >80% of high-end AI packaging but capacity constraints prevent full market capture. OSATs benefit from spillover rather than direct competition.

## No evidence of TSMC vertical integration into substrates or equipment

**Evidence Found:** Contrary to the kill condition, TSMC shows no indication of integrating into substrate manufacturing or bonding equipment production. The company explicitly partners with, rather than displaces, independent suppliers.

**Substrates:** TSMC describes an "integrated turnkey service" achieved through "intense collaboration with substrate, memory and materials suppliers" (TSMC official website). The ABF substrate market remains dominated by **Ibiden, Shinko, Unimicron, and Nan Ya PCB**, with no TSMC presence. A persistent **20% supply-demand gap** exists in ABF substrates with lead times exceeding 35 weeks, and spot-price premiums reached 25% in 2025. Substrate makers are supply-constrained with pricing power—the opposite of being squeezed.

**Bonding Equipment:** TSMC uses external equipment suppliers for all hybrid bonding: **Besi, EV Group, Applied Materials, and ASMPT**. Far from competing with Besi, **Applied Materials acquired a 9% stake in Besi in April 2025**, becoming its largest shareholder—indicating consolidation rather than competition. Besi maintains a near-monopoly in die-to-wafer hybrid bonding, with Yole Group analyst Vishal Saroha stating "Besi is the frontrunner in this segment... It's an open secret."

**TSMC Strategy:** CEO C.C. Wei's Q3 2025 earnings commentary emphasized partnership: "We are working with one OSAT, a big company and our good partner [Amkor], and they are going to build their fab in Arizona." TSMC explicitly outsources lower-margin substrate assembly (WoS portion) while retaining high-margin silicon interposer and Chip-on-Wafer processes.

**Kill Condition Assessment:** **NOT TRIGGERED** — No evidence of vertical integration. TSMC depends on independent substrate and equipment suppliers.

## OSAT margins show mixed signals—operating compression, gross stability

**Evidence Found:** The margin compression kill condition shows nuanced results. Operating margins are clearly compressing due to investment needs, but gross margins have stabilized, suggesting some pricing power retention.

**Amkor Financial Performance (2023-2025):**
- Gross margin: 14.5% (FY2023) → **14.8%** (FY2024) → ~13.4% projected (FY2025)
- Operating margin: 7.2% (FY2023) → **6.9%** (FY2024) — declining
- Revenue: **Declining** — $6.50B (2023) → $6.32B (2024)
- Advanced packaging now **82%** of revenue (up from 77% in 2023)

**ASE Technology Performance (2023-2025):**
- Consolidated gross margin: 15.8% (FY2023) → **16.3%** (FY2024) — improving
- Operating margin: 6.9% (FY2023) → **6.6%** (FY2024) — declining
- ATM segment gross margin: 21.8% → **22.5%** — improving
- ATM operating margin: 10.1% → **9.8%** — declining
- LEAP (advanced packaging) revenue: $250M (2023) → $600M (2024) → **$1.6B projected (2025)** — +167% growth

**Critical Pattern:** Both companies show **operating margin compression** (due to increased R&D/SG&A to compete with TSMC) while **gross margins stabilize or improve slightly**. ASE CFO Joseph Tung acknowledged in Q3 2024: "We are a bit disappointed with this year's margin performance." However, management attributed this partly to currency headwinds (4.2pp NT$ appreciation impact) and ramp costs for advanced packaging.

**Structural Bifurcation Emerging:** TSMC retains high-margin CoWoS steps (~50-80% gross margin) while outsourcing lower-margin substrate assembly (35-45% margin) to OSATs. Industry analysts characterize this as "margin stratification" rather than pure compression.

**Kill Condition Assessment:** **PARTIALLY TRIGGERED** — Operating margin compression confirmed for 2+ consecutive years. However, gross margin stability and LEAP revenue growth suggest OSATs retain some pricing power in specific segments.

## Conclusion: thesis survives with qualifications

The falsification exercise reveals that **the investment thesis is NOT invalidated** but requires significant qualification:

**What the evidence supports:**
1. OSATs (Amkor, ASE) will capture value from advanced packaging—primarily through **overflow demand and lower-margin segments**, not by competing head-to-head with TSMC
2. Substrate suppliers (Ibiden, Shinko) maintain critical position with **pricing power** due to supply constraints; no TSMC competition
3. Equipment vendors (Besi) hold strong position in hybrid bonding with **TSMC/Intel as customers**, not competitors
4. Customer diversification is **real and documented**—NVIDIA, AMD actively qualifying OSATs

**What the evidence challenges:**
1. OSATs will NOT capture "significant value" from the highest-margin advanced packaging work—TSMC controls ~85% of AI chip packaging
2. Operating margin compression is real and ongoing
3. OSAT role is structurally subordinate to TSMC's strategic decisions—industry experts call it a "one-player game"

**Modified Thesis:** Independent ecosystem players benefit from the advanced packaging shift, but as **complementary overflow providers** rather than direct competitors to TSMC. The value capture is real but concentrated in lower-margin segments. Substrate suppliers and equipment vendors face better structural dynamics than OSATs due to supply constraints and lack of direct TSMC competition.
