# Evidence Resolution: National Resilience Investment Theme

## Research Findings

### Unknown 1: Is National Resilience Spending Genuinely Incremental?

#### Falsification Track

**Research Date**: 2026-03-24
**Objective**: Find evidence that spending is repackaged, not incremental

**Kill Condition Assessment**:
> Total government defense + infrastructure spending as % of GDP unchanged; bilateral agreements non-binding; JOGMEC/JBIC disbursements flat or declining; US defense budget at CBO baseline; no private sector CapEx acceleration.

**Status**: PARTIALLY TRIGGERED (4 of 5 conditions at least partially met)

**Key Findings**:

1. **Japan's 2% target is accounting reclassification** — Actual MOD budget targets ~1.5% of GDP; gap filled by reclassifying Coast Guard, infrastructure, pensions under "Comprehensive Defense Architecture." SIPRI confirms ¥8.9T = 1.5% of GDP, not 2%.
   - Source: Brookings Institution, SIPRI
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Directly supports repackaging kill condition

2. **JBIC disbursements collapsed 42% since FY2020** — Total commitments fell from ¥2.6T to ¥1.5T. Natural resource loans down 18.5% YoY. No upward trend in critical minerals specifically.
   - Source: JBIC annual reports
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Directly triggers JOGMEC/JBIC kill condition

3. **Bilateral agreements are non-binding frameworks** — Sullivan & Cromwell described the critical minerals agreement as a "non-binding common policy framework." The $550B "investment pledge" is private-sector commitments, not government appropriations.
   - Source: Sullivan & Cromwell legal analysis, RUSI
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Directly triggers bilateral agreement kill condition

4. **US base defense budget flat in real terms** — FY2026 $892.6B identical to FY2025 enacted. 5th consecutive year flat-to-declining. The "$1T headline" relies on one-time reconciliation funding. Without supplement, R&D would decrease 18%.
   - Source: CBO, Senate Armed Services Committee
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Directly triggers US spending kill condition

5. **Biden-era resilience apparatus being dismantled** — DOE LPO lost 75% of funding and 58.5% of staff. $11B+ in IRA funds rescinded. $6.8B in BIL awards cancelled. Trump called CHIPS Act "horrible."
   - Source: Congressional records, executive actions
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Active political rollback contradicts "survives electoral cycles"

6. **Private sector CapEx driven by AI hype, not resilience** — Palantir's growth is AI Platform commercial (121% YoY) not government. Anduril is VC-funded at $1B+ losses. Defense primes show routine, formulaic CapEx.
   - Source: Company filings
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Partially triggers private sector CapEx condition

**Caveats**: Adversarial framing deprioritized genuine pockets of new spending. Critical minerals stockpiling (US $2B NDS) is real. European aggregate defense has increased in nominal terms.

#### Validation Track

**Research Date**: 2026-03-24
**Objective**: Identify confirmation signals and leading indicators

**Conviction Condition Assessment**:
> Net new appropriations verifiable; bilateral agreements binding; JOGMEC/JBIC step-change; private sector CapEx citing resilience incentives; coordinated multi-country increases.

**Status**: PARTIALLY MET — stronger institutional lock-in than falsification captured

**Key Findings**:

1. **Germany's constitutional debt brake reform is the strongest lock-in globally** — Bundestag voted 512-206 to amend Basic Law, permanently exempting defense above 1% GDP. €500B infrastructure fund created. 2025 budget €86.5B (+20.5% YoY), 2026 approved at ~€108B. Reversing requires two-thirds supermajority.
   - Source: Bundestag records, German government
   - Credibility: High
   - Mechanism: Constitutional amendment is the highest form of democratic lock-in

2. **$500B+ sunk semiconductor capital creates irreversible constituency** — TSMC $165B Arizona (Fab 1 operational), Micron $200B, Samsung $44B, Intel $28B + $8.9B government equity. Trump enhanced CHIPS tax credit from 25% to 35%.
   - Source: SIA, company announcements
   - Credibility: High
   - Mechanism: F-35 model — spread across 21 states, 125K+ jobs makes cancellation politically irrational

3. **DOD equity stakes in critical minerals are unprecedented** — $400M convertible preferred in MP Materials (15% ownership), $150M loan, 10-year price floor at $110/kg, 10-year offtake. Government-as-shareholder makes cancellation self-destructive.
   - Source: DOD announcements, MP Materials filings
   - Credibility: High
   - Mechanism: Direct government equity creates different political calculus than grants

4. **Japan's defense increased 67% in four years** — ¥5.4T (FY2022) to ¥9.04T (FY2026). 2% achieved 2 years early per Defense Minister. ¥43T five-year program tracking on schedule. Even if partially reclassified, the MOD increase is real.
   - Source: Japanese government budget, Defense Minister statements
   - Credibility: High
   - Mechanism: Structural break from 46-year 1% cap

5. **NATO 32/32 at 2%, EU SAFE €150B defense bonds** — First time all allies meet target. EU mutualized defense debt for first time via 45-year bonds. €38B first disbursement approved.
   - Source: NATO reports, EU Council
   - Credibility: High
   - Mechanism: Joint debt issuance creates institutional infrastructure for ongoing borrowing

6. **Multi-threat simultaneity resembles early Cold War** — China + Russia + Iran + supply chains simultaneously. Historical pattern: multi-threat environments produce more durable plateaus than single-threat episodes. Eisenhower couldn't reverse Truman's buildup.
   - Source: Historical analysis, academic literature
   - Credibility: Medium-high
   - Mechanism: Demobilization on one front impossible when others remain active

7. **12 specific confirmation signals identified** — Each with data source, current baseline, trigger threshold, and monitoring frequency. Several already partially triggered (Germany >€100B, Japan 2%, NATO 32/32).

**Caveats**: Validation framing may overweight positive evidence. Fiscal constraints are severe and worsening across all countries. "Sunk cost" argument could be countered by Foxconn/Solyndra precedents.

### Unknown 3: Do Crisis-Driven Spending Commitments Historically Persist?

#### Falsification Track

**Research Date**: 2026-03-24
**Objective**: Find evidence that commitments historically fade

**Kill Condition Assessment**:
> >60% of crisis-driven commitments walked back within 3 years; post-Crimea majority failed 2% within 5 years; no institutional lock-in mechanisms; Japan shows announcement without follow-through.

**Status**: PARTIALLY TRIGGERED

**Key Findings**:

1. **100% of US defense buildups since WWII reverted** — Korea (15% → 10%), Vietnam (9.5% → 5.5%), Reagan (6.8% → 3.0%), post-9/11 (5.7% → 3.1%). Each successive peak lower than the last. Sequestration cut even post-9/11 spending despite active combat.
   - Source: SIPRI, CSIS, official budget data
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: 0-for-6 historical record on sustained buildups

2. **Post-Crimea: 7/30 (23%) at 2% after 8 years** — Only direct-threat countries (Baltics, Poland) sustained increases. Western Europe ignored pledges until 2022 full-scale invasion. Real NATO CAGR 2014-2022 was 1.3%.
   - Source: NATO official data, Carnegie Endowment
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Most relevant modern analogue shows spending-pledge failure

3. **Current commitments lack post-9/11 institutional architecture** — No new permanent cabinet-level agency. No standing military authorization. No active combat operations. CHIPS Program Office lost ~120 of 150 staff to DOGE.
   - Source: Legal analysis, congressional records
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Supports absence of lock-in kill condition

4. **Japan's ESPA implementation lags years behind rhetoric** — Enacted May 2022, key pillars not operational until May 2024. Rare earth dependency on China increased from 50% to 70%. Defense tax delayed from 2024 to 2027. 1% cap survived 46 years despite repeated pledges.
   - Source: Brookings, Columbia/CJEB, ANU
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Partially supports Japan follow-through kill condition

5. **CHIPS Act: 95% gap between commitment and disbursement** — $1.1B disbursed vs $39B manufacturing incentives. Intel Ohio delayed to 2030-31. Samsung Taylor has no equipment or customers. TSMC 1-2 years behind. EU had same 20% target in 2013, achieved 3%.
   - Source: Government disbursement data, European Court of Auditors
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Enormous announcement-to-delivery gap

6. **European 2028 fiscal cliff** — 17 member states activated SGP escape clause temporarily. Expires ~2028. 8 already in Excessive Deficit Procedures. Defense spending ratchet effect weaker than for social spending — no mass beneficiary constituency.
   - Source: EU Council, Bruegel analysis, academic literature
   - Credibility: High
   - Bearing: Structural fiscal constraint on durability

**Caveats**: Adversarial framing. Multi-threat convergence is genuinely unprecedented. Active Ukraine war maintains threat salience unlike post-Crimea. Germany's constitutional reform is genuinely structural. "Within 3 years" reversion threshold may be too narrow — reversions typically take 5-10 years.

## Synthesis (by Theme)

### Theme 1: The Repackaging Problem Is Real but Not Total

The falsification found devastating evidence that headline spending figures are inflated through accounting reclassification. Japan's 2% is ~1.5% actual MOD. NATO's 5% invites creative accounting (Italy's bridge, UK's war pensions, Germany's interest payments). The US "$1T defense budget" relies on one-time reconciliation money.

**However**, the validation found this is not the complete picture. Germany's constitutional amendment is genuine — €86.5B rising to €108B in real procurement contracts worth €188B over three years. Japan's 67% MOD increase is real even if the "2%" label is inflated. European aggregate defense doubled in six years. The repackaging exists alongside genuine incremental spending.

- Supported by: All three research files
- Strength: **Strong** — both repackaging and genuine incremental spending are empirically verified
- Impact: **Modifies thesis** — spending is incrementally higher but the magnitude is overstated by headlines

### Theme 2: Institutional Lock-In Is Emerging but Unevenly

The falsification found no DHS-equivalent agencies, no binding treaties, and heavy reliance on annual appropriations. The validation found different *forms* of lock-in that didn't exist historically: constitutional amendments (Germany), government equity stakes (DOD in MP Materials, Intel), sunk private capital ($500B+ semiconductors), regulatory frameworks (EU CRMA), and mutualized defense debt (EU SAFE bonds).

These are not the same lock-in mechanisms as post-9/11 — they're more distributed and structural, less centralized and agency-based. Whether they're as durable is genuinely unknown.

- Supported by: Unknown 1 validation (findings 1-5), Unknown 3 falsification (finding 3)
- Strength: **Moderate** — mechanisms exist but are untested against fiscal consolidation pressure
- Impact: **Supports conditional thesis** — lock-in exists in specific domains, not across-the-board

### Theme 3: Fiscal Gravity Is the Binding Constraint

Both falsification tracks converge on this: US debt at 100%+ GDP with interest exceeding defense; Japan at 240% debt-to-GDP; EU escape clauses expiring 2028. The honest assessment from validation is spending at a "modest plateau above recent baselines" (3.5-4% US, 2.5-3% Europe) — not the dramatic step-change Aitken's language implies.

Every historical defense buildup was eventually reversed by fiscal consolidation. The question is whether the current multi-threat environment delays this reversion long enough (through 2030+) for the thesis to play out.

- Supported by: Unknown 1 falsification (finding 6), Unknown 3 falsification (findings 6-7), Unknown 1 validation (finding on fiscal vulnerability)
- Strength: **Strong** — fiscal arithmetic is unambiguous
- Impact: **Constrains thesis magnitude** — spending plateau, not exponential compounding

### Theme 4: Multi-Threat Simultaneity Is the Differentiating Factor

The validation's strongest argument: China + Russia + Iran + supply chains simultaneously is structurally unprecedented since the early Cold War. Historical pattern shows multi-threat environments produce more durable spending than single-threat episodes. Post-Crimea failed because it was a single-theater threat; the current moment is multi-theater.

The falsification counters that even post-9/11 (with active combat, new agency, standing AUMF) was reversed by sequestration. But the validation argues post-9/11 was fundamentally single-threat (terrorism), while the current convergence prevents sequential demobilization.

- Supported by: Unknown 1 validation (finding 6, signal 1), Unknown 3 falsification (caveats)
- Strength: **Moderate** — compelling logical argument, limited direct evidence (only Cold War analogue)
- Impact: **Supports monitoring thesis** — if threats persist simultaneously, historical base rate may not apply

### Theme 5: Pockets of Genuine New Capital Are Identifiable

Beneath the noise of headline repackaging, specific domains show verifiable incremental spending:
- **Critical minerals**: DOD equity in MP Materials, NDS $2B stockpile, EU CRMA projects
- **European defense**: Germany's procurement pipeline (€188B in 3 years vs €109B in prior 8), EU SAFE €150B bonds
- **Semiconductor onshoring**: TSMC Fab 1 operational, enhanced tax credits, $500B+ committed
- **Japan defense hardware**: ¥43T five-year program, standoff missiles, GCAP fighter

These pockets are real, verifiable, and investable — even if the broad "national resilience compounding" narrative is overstated.

- Supported by: Unknown 1 validation (findings 1-5, 7)
- Strength: **Strong** — specific, verifiable, with named projects and dollar amounts
- Impact: **Supports narrower thesis** — investable in specific domains, not as broad thematic trade
