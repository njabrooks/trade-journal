# China copper demand may delay but not kill the deficit thesis

The copper supply deficit by 2027 faces a **partial falsification risk** from China's demand trajectory—not the crushing blow from property sector weakness that the counter-thesis assumes. Two consecutive months of severe import declines (October-November 2025 at -16% to -23% YoY) approach the kill condition threshold, yet the fundamental offset assumption—that property releases 1-2 Mt/year of copper—is **overstated by nearly 10x**. Goldman Sachs now forecasts surpluses persisting until **2029**, pushing the deficit timeline back but not eliminating it.

## The kill condition is approaching but not yet triggered

The specified falsification threshold—China imports declining >10% YoY for three consecutive months alongside SHFE inventories rising >200kt—has not been fully met, though signals are increasingly bearish.

**Import decline status:** Copper cathode imports fell **-23.47% YoY** in November 2025 and **-16.32% YoY** in October, following a dramatic reversal from the +11-12% YoY growth seen in July-August. Full-year 2025 unwrought copper imports dropped **6.4%** to 5.32 million tonnes—the lowest since 2020. This represents two consecutive months exceeding the 10% threshold; a third (December 2025) would trigger the condition. Goldman Sachs estimates Chinese refined copper demand collapsed **-8% YoY in Q4 2025**, with rod makers operating at just **61% capacity**—the lowest October reading in nearly a decade.

**Inventory position:** SHFE copper stocks rose from a July 2025 low of **81,462 tonnes** to **148,193 tonnes** in January 2026—an increase of approximately **67,000 tonnes**, only one-third of the 200kt threshold. This rise is materially smaller than the kill condition requires. However, COMEX inventories exploded to **21-year highs above 514,000 tonnes** (up 388% from end-2024), and combined global exchange inventories rose approximately 68% from mid-2025 to January 2026.

## Property sector copper release is 10x smaller than assumed

The counter-thesis assumption that property weakness releases **1-2 Mt/year** of copper demand is **not supported by available evidence**. Documented property copper decline is approximately **100,000-130,000 tonnes annually** according to Minmetals Economic Research Institute—an order of magnitude smaller.

China's construction and property sector now accounts for roughly **20-25% of domestic copper consumption** (down from ~30% historically), translating to approximately **2.5-3 million tonnes annually**. Even a catastrophic 40% decline in property copper demand would release only 1-1.2 Mt—matching the lower bound of the counter-thesis assumption only under extreme scenarios. Floor space completions fell -19.1% YoY through mid-2025, yet grid infrastructure alone added **180,000-200,000 tonnes** of incremental demand—fully offsetting property losses.

The structural demand substitution is real but favors the bull case: new energy's share of China copper demand jumped from **6.8% in 2021 to 19.4% in 2025**, representing approximately 2 million tonnes of demand shifting to growth sectors. Power grid investment hit **$80-100 billion in 2025** (a new annual record), consuming roughly 5.7 million tonnes of copper annually. EV copper demand reached **1.7 Mt in 2025** with a projected **14.3% CAGR** through 2034.

## Goldman Sachs delays the deficit to 2029

The most credible institutional bearish view comes from Goldman Sachs, which forecasts the global copper market will remain in **surplus through 2028**:

- **2025:** 500,000 tonne surplus
- **2026:** 160,000 tonne surplus  
- **Deficit timing:** "From 2029 onwards"

Goldman analyst Eoin Dinsmore stated explicitly: "We do not expect the global copper market to enter a shortage any time soon." This directly contradicts the 2027 deficit timeline. The bank projects copper prices capped at **$10,000-$11,000/tonne** in 2026 due to persistent surplus conditions.

ICSG forecasts align more closely with the original thesis—predicting a **150,000 tonne deficit in 2026** after revising from surplus expectations following mine disruptions at Grasberg and Kamoa. J.P. Morgan projects a **330,000 tonne deficit in 2026** with prices reaching $12,500/mt. This institutional disagreement reveals genuine uncertainty about timing.

## AI and data center copper demand is currently immaterial

Data centers represent only **1-1.5% of current global copper consumption**—approximately 467,000 tonnes in 2023 rising to a projected 710,000 tonnes by 2026. This is material growth but remains a small slice of the **27+ million tonne annual market**. BMO Capital Markets analyst Colin Hamilton notes that "data centers themselves are becoming incrementally less copper-intensive"—the real copper intensity comes from supporting grid infrastructure, not data center internals.

The copper-intensity argument for AI may be running ahead of actual demand materialization. Current data center copper consumption at **4-5% of insulated wire/cable demand** is meaningful but not transformational. BHP estimates data center copper demand at approximately 0.5 million tonnes today, rising to 3 million by 2050—a gradual trajectory rather than an imminent inflection point.

## Aluminum substitution presents a structural headwind

The copper-to-aluminum price ratio surged past **4.2x** in January 2026, breaching a historically significant substitution trigger. Goldman Sachs expects the ratio to reach a **new high of 4.5:1** in 2026. This has accelerated material switching across multiple sectors:

- **HVAC:** "Once you redesign for aluminum, customers don't go back to using copper" (Hydro Precision Tubing VP)
- **Automotive:** Aluminum wiring harnesses reduce EV weight by 40%
- **Power transmission:** High-voltage transmission already completely switched decades ago

Aluminum is one-third the price, one-third the weight, and **1,200x more abundant** in Earth's crust than copper. The permanence of substitution once engineering redesigns occur represents a structural headwind to copper demand growth projections.

## The conviction-increase condition shows mixed signals

The bullish trigger—China imports remaining flat-to-positive despite property weakness AND global inventories declining—has **not been met**. Imports fell sharply in Q4 2025, and total global exchange inventories rose 68% from mid-2025 driven by COMEX accumulation.

However, context matters: LME inventories dropped **58% year-to-date** to multi-year lows around 114,000 tonnes, and SHFE remains historically tight. The COMEX buildup reflects tariff anticipation and trade flow distortion rather than fundamental surplus. If tariff uncertainty resolves, this inventory could re-enter global markets.

## Assessment: Thesis weakened but not killed

The evidence suggests **modifying rather than killing** the copper deficit thesis:

**Falsification strength: Moderate.** The kill condition has not been triggered, but two key bearish signals—consecutive 10%+ import declines and rising global inventories—indicate demand weakness is more severe than bulls projected. Goldman Sachs' 2029 deficit timeline represents a credible 2-3 year delay from the original 2027 thesis.

**Property offset magnitude: Overstated.** The counter-thesis assumption of 1-2 Mt/year property copper release appears to be 10x too high. Actual property decline (~100-130kt/year) is being offset by grid and EV growth.

**Price implications:** A delayed deficit (2029 vs 2027) would not support near-term 30%+ price increases. Goldman's $10,000-$11,000/tonne forecast implies limited upside from current levels through 2026-2027.

| 30%+ price increase | **Weakened** | Moderate (surplus caps prices) |

| AI driving deficit | **Too early** | Moderate (currently 1-1.5% of demand) |

## Conclusion

The copper deficit thesis survives but requires timeline revision. China's property weakness is releasing approximately **100,000-200,000 tonnes annually**—meaningful but insufficient to offset combined grid, EV, and renewable demand growth exceeding **1.5 million tonnes annually**. The most significant falsification evidence is Goldman Sachs' surplus forecast through 2028 and the two consecutive months of >10% YoY import declines. If December 2025 data confirms a third consecutive month of severe decline, the kill condition would be triggered, signaling demand destruction overwhelming AI additions and delaying deficits to 2029 or beyond.
