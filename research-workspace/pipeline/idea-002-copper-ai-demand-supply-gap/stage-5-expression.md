## Order of Effects

### First-Order (Direct Exposure)

**Copper miners are the most direct expression of this thesis.**

- **Freeport-McMoRan (FCX)**
  - Why first-order: Largest US copper producer, ~3.9 Mt production capacity, Grasberg world-class asset
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Cost inflation, labor disputes, tax changes in Indonesia
  - Consensus level: **moderate** - widely held but not extreme positioning

- **Southern Copper (SCCO)**
  - Why first-order: Lowest cost producer globally (~$0.90/lb cash cost), Peru/Mexico assets
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Peru political instability, resource nationalism
  - Consensus level: **moderate** - known as quality but valuation typically premium

- **Copper ETF (COPX)**
  - Why first-order: Broad miner exposure without single-stock risk
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Index composition, tracking error
  - Consensus level: **underfollowed** - less attention than single names

### Second-Order (Derivative Demand)

**Infrastructure enablers benefit from buildout regardless of copper price.**

- **Grid equipment manufacturers (EATON, Schneider)**
  - Why second-order: Increased transformer/switchgear demand from data center buildout
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Copper cost pass-through risk, competition
  - Why potentially better than first-order: Less commodity volatility, steadier earnings

- **Copper recyclers/scrap processors**
  - Why second-order: Spread between refined and scrap widens in tight markets
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Collection rates, processing capacity
  - Why potentially better than first-order: Faster supply response, lower capital intensity

### Third-Order (Enablers/Infrastructure)

**Long-duration plays on structural copper demand shift.**

- **Mining equipment (Caterpillar)**
  - Why third-order: Eventual mine capex cycle if deficit materializes
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Miners delay capex, technology shift
  - Duration alignment: 3-5 year cycle for mine development

- **Chile/Peru country exposure (via ETFs)**
  - Why third-order: National revenue exposure to copper
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Political risk, currency, diversification
  - Duration alignment: Very long-duration, indirect

### Potential Shorts (Victims)

**Caution: Timing uncertain given Goldman's 2029 deficit forecast.**

- **High-cost copper miners**
  - Why victim: If prices stay range-bound through 2028, high-cost producers struggle
  - Risk if shorting: M&A activity, production cuts making them scarce
  - Timing consideration: Not near-term - surplus conditions protect them temporarily

- **Aluminum substitution NOT a short**
  - Note: Substitution is gradual and creates demand for both metals during transition
  - Better expressed as reduced copper upside than aluminum long
