## Order of Effects

### First-Order (Direct Exposure)

- **SpaceX/xAI (private)**
  - Why first-order: THE direct play — owns launch, manufacturing, and compute demand
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Private company; no public equity expression. Secondary market shares are illiquid, opaque, and subject to lockups. IPO timeline unknown.
  - Consensus level: moderate — orbital compute thesis is gaining attention post-FCC filing, but most investors still price SpaceX as a launch/Starlink company, not an orbital compute platform

- **Tesla (TSLA)**
  - Why first-order: Primary public market proxy for SpaceX economics. Shared Musk ecosystem narrative — SpaceX milestones (Starship reuse, orbital DC deployment) directly boost Musk credibility and TSLA narrative. Tesla Energy (solar + battery) benefits from broader awareness of solar economics. Optimus robotics thesis also originates from same Musk interview.
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Proxy risk is high. TSLA has its own thesis drivers (auto margins, FSD, Optimus, energy) that can decouple from SpaceX. If TSLA auto business deteriorates, it could fall even as SpaceX thrives. Valuation already stretched at ~100× forward earnings — orbital compute is a narrative add-on, not a primary driver.
  - Consensus level: crowded — TSLA is one of the most traded stocks globally; Musk ecosystem thesis is well-known

### Second-Order (Derivative Demand)

- **Mynaric (MYNA) / Optical communications pure-plays**
  - Why second-order: Every orbital data center needs optical inter-satellite links AND space-to-ground links. Demand is proportional to satellite count regardless of who wins the orbital compute market. Already supplying Starlink and SDA Tranche programs.
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Tiny companies with execution risk; SpaceX may vertically integrate optical systems (as they did with Starlink laser links)
  - Why potentially better than first-order: Less crowded; direct revenue linkage to satellite count; benefits regardless of which orbital compute provider wins

- **Solestial (private) / Space solar panel innovators**
  - Why second-order: Claims 10× lower cost than traditional space solar ($30/W vs $300/W). If space solar becomes competitive, demand scales with orbital DC deployment.
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Private; unproven at scale; may be acquired before generating independent returns
  - Why potentially better than first-order: Addresses the specific cost bottleneck (solar panel $/W) that determines when orbital compute crosses the economic threshold

### Third-Order (Enablers/Infrastructure)

- **Astroscale (6 TYO:186A) / Orbital debris management**
  - Why third-order: A million-satellite orbital DC constellation creates existential-level debris risk. Regulatory bodies will mandate debris mitigation, creating a structural demand floor. But timing is very long (5-10yr).
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Business model unproven; regulation may not mandate commercial debris services
  - Duration alignment: Very long — matches the 5-8yr+ thesis horizon

- **Ground station infrastructure (AWS Ground Station, KSAT)**
  - Why third-order: Data backhaul from orbit to ground requires massive ground station network expansion. Picks-and-shovels play.
  - Risk if thesis right but expression fails: Amazon (AMZN) and Azure already operate ground stations; may not create new public market investment opportunities
  - Duration alignment: 3-5yr — infrastructure must be built ahead of orbital DC deployment

### Potential Shorts (Victims)

- **Terrestrial data center REITs (EQIX, DLR)** — on a 5-10yr horizon
  - Why victim: Moat is proximity to power and fiber. If AI training migrates partially to orbit, their fastest-growing demand segment erodes.
  - Risk if shorting: These are profitable, dividend-paying businesses with long-duration contracts. The terrestrial market won't disappear — only the marginal growth rate declines. Short thesis requires very long horizon (5-10yr) with significant carry cost.
  - Timing consideration: Not actionable until orbital compute is demonstrably operational at scale (likely not before 2030)

- **Pure-play terrestrial GPU cloud (COREWEAVE)**
  - Why victim: 100% terrestrial with massive capex commitments. If orbital compute becomes 2× cheaper by 2032, their infrastructure becomes a stranded asset.
  - Risk if shorting: CoreWeave is private (IPO expected). If they IPO, timing of short matters — they'll generate strong near-term revenue from AI demand. Thesis-based short requires 5+ year horizon.
  - Timing consideration: Too early to short. The terrestrial power constraint actually helps them in the near term.
