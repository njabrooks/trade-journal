## Top 3 Unknowns (Detailed Analysis)

### Unknown 1: Supply Response Capacity at $4-5/MMBTU

**Decision Impact**: HIGH

This is the **core thesis risk**. If US shale gas can ramp production fast enough at $4-5/MMBTU to meet incremental demand, prices normalize and the thesis fails. The supply elasticity question determines whether this is a structural re-rating or a temporary spike.

**Resolution Type**: empirical + industry

**Externally Resolvable**: yes

The data exists: basin-level production data, rig count economics, producer breakevens, infrastructure capacity constraints. This is knowable.

**Kill Condition**:
- Haynesville + Marcellus have >20 BCF/day of readily accessible capacity at $4/MMBTU breakeven
- Producer earnings calls indicate "growth mode" with 10%+ production guidance increases
- Gas rig counts can increase 50%+ within 12 months without labor/equipment constraints
- Pipeline/gathering capacity exists to move incremental production to demand centers

**Conviction Increase Condition**:
- Basin decline rates are accelerating, requiring more drilling just to maintain production
- Labor and equipment constraints cap rig count growth at <20% annually
- Pipeline bottlenecks in Appalachia limit Marcellus takeaway capacity
- Producer capital discipline holds despite price signals (debt covenants, investor pressure)

**Recommended Sources**:
- **EIA Natural Gas Weekly/Monthly**: Production by basin, rig counts, storage levels
- **Baker Hughes Rig Count**: Weekly gas-directed rig trends by basin
- **Producer earnings transcripts** (EQT, Antero, Comstock, Southwestern): Breakeven guidance, growth plans, capital allocation
- **Midstream company filings** (Williams, Kinder Morgan): Pipeline capacity utilization, expansion plans
- **BTU Analytics / East Daley**: Basin-level supply models and infrastructure analysis

**Estimated Effort**: 6-8 hours

**Research Queries**:
1. What is the current Haynesville and Marcellus production capacity vs. actual production? What's the "spare capacity"?
2. What are stated producer breakevens by basin, and how have they changed with inflation?
3. What are the pipeline capacity constraints out of Appalachia (Marcellus) and how long to resolve?
4. What did producers do during the 2022 price spike? Did supply respond? Why or why not?
5. What is the current gas-directed rig count vs. peak, and what are equipment/labor constraints?

### Unknown 2: Are All Three Demand Pillars Actually Converging?

**Decision Impact**: HIGH

The thesis claims three independent megatrends are converging simultaneously. But how real is each pillar? AI data center demand gets headlines but what's the actual TWh impact? Is electrification actually accelerating or plateauing? Is LNG export demand durable or cyclical?

**Resolution Type**: empirical

**Externally Resolvable**: yes

Each pillar can be quantified separately. EIA publishes electricity demand data. Data center power consumption is trackable. LNG export volumes are public. This is measurable.

**Kill Condition**:
- AI data center electricity demand is <3% of incremental demand growth (noise, not signal)
- US electricity demand growth remains <2% annually through 2025-2026 (trifecta not materializing)
- LNG export growth stalls due to European demand destruction or alternative supply (Qatar, Australia)
- EV adoption plateaus and industrial electrification slows

**Conviction Increase Condition**:
- EIA upwardly revises electricity demand forecasts to >3% annually
- Hyperscaler power demand grows >30% YoY with visible grid connection queues
- LNG export utilization rates exceed 90% with contracted volumes extending to 2030+
- Multiple independent sources confirm demand acceleration (not just one optimistic forecast)

**Recommended Sources**:
- **EIA Electric Power Monthly**: Actual electricity demand data (not forecasts)
- **EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook**: Demand forecasts and revisions
- **JP Morgan "Electrovision" research** (Michael Cembalest): Comprehensive demand analysis
- **Hyperscaler earnings calls** (MSFT, META, GOOGL, AMZN): Data center capex and power demand guidance
- **LNG export data** (EIA, Cheniere filings): Utilization rates, contracted volumes

**Estimated Effort**: 4-6 hours

**Research Queries**:
1. What is actual US electricity demand growth in 2024-2025? Has it accelerated from the 0.5% baseline?
2. What percentage of incremental electricity demand is attributable to data centers vs. electrification vs. other?
3. What are current LNG export volumes and utilization rates? What's contracted vs. spot?
4. How have EIA demand forecasts changed over the past 3 years? Consistently revised up or volatile?
5. What are hyperscalers saying about power constraints in earnings calls?

### Unknown 3: Timeline and Capacity for Nuclear Restarts

**Decision Impact**: HIGH

Nuclear restarts represent the most credible near-term alternative to natural gas for baseload. If 5-10 GW of nuclear capacity restarts by 2028, that directly offsets natural gas demand growth. The Microsoft/Three Mile Island deal suggests this is real.

**Resolution Type**: industry + regulatory

**Externally Resolvable**: partially

Restart candidates are known (Palisades, Three Mile Island, potentially others). NRC timelines are somewhat predictable. But execution risk and cost overruns are harder to assess externally.

**Kill Condition**:
- >5 nuclear restart projects announced with credible 2027-2028 timelines
- NRC fast-tracks restart approvals (new regulatory posture)
- Total restart capacity exceeds 8 GW (meaningful vs. ~100 GW existing nuclear fleet)
- SMR projects achieve commercial operation (not just NRC approval)

**Conviction Increase Condition**:
- Nuclear restarts limited to 2-3 projects (Palisades, TMI) with <4 GW total capacity
- NRC maintains cautious posture with 3-5 year restart timelines
- Cost overruns plague restart projects (Vogtle precedent)
- SMRs remain in demonstration phase through 2028

**Recommended Sources**:
- **NRC filings and announcements**: Restart applications, approval timelines
- **Utility filings** (Holtec for Palisades, Constellation for TMI): Project status, cost estimates
- **DOE loan program announcements**: Federal support for restarts
- **Nuclear Energy Institute**: Industry advocacy and pipeline tracking
- **S&P Global / Power Engineering**: Nuclear project tracking

**Estimated Effort**: 3-4 hours

**Research Queries**:
1. Which nuclear plants are currently in restart process and what are realistic online dates?
2. What is total potential restart capacity in the US (plants that could theoretically restart)?
3. What does NRC's current posture on restarts look like? Any policy changes?
4. What is the cost per MW for restarts vs. greenfield nuclear vs. new gas plants?
5. What is the current status of SMR projects (NuScale, others)? When could they contribute meaningful capacity?

## Gate Assessment

**Decision**: advance

**Rationale**:

This thesis passes Stage 3 criteria decisively:

1. **High-impact unknowns exist**: All three top unknowns are HIGH impact. Resolving any one of them could significantly change conviction or kill the thesis.

2. **Unknowns are externally resolvable**:
   - Unknown 1 (supply response): YES - EIA data, producer filings, rig counts are all public
   - Unknown 2 (demand convergence): YES - electricity demand data, LNG volumes, hyperscaler disclosures
   - Unknown 3 (nuclear timeline): PARTIALLY - restart candidates known, but execution risk harder to assess

3. **Clear kill conditions exist**: Each unknown has specific, measurable kill conditions:
   - >20 BCF/day spare capacity at $4 breakeven
   - Electricity demand growth <2% annually
   - >5 restart projects with 2027-2028 timelines

4. **Payoff is asymmetric**:
   - If thesis is right: Natural gas structural re-rating offers multi-year tailwind for gas producers, LNG, midstream
   - Research cost: ~15-20 hours total
   - This is worth the research investment

**Why this passes (not kills)**:
- The unknowns are genuinely uncertain (not obviously answered)
- The data sources are accessible and credible
- The thesis mechanism is sound; we're testing assumptions, not the logic
- Multiple expression vehicles exist if thesis validates (UNG, individual E&Ps, midstream, LNG)
