# The demand convergence thesis faces serious headwinds

**The three pillars supporting the US natural gas demand acceleration thesis—AI data centers, electrification, and LNG exports—are not converging as strongly as bulls claim.** One kill condition is definitively met (electrification plateau), one is partially met (European LNG demand destruction), and the others show significant structural weakness despite not being conclusively falsified. The 2.5-5% electricity demand growth projection appears unrealistic; actual growth is tracking closer to 2%, with forecasts being revised *down*, not up.

---

## Kill condition scorecard shows mixed but concerning signals

The four kill conditions specified for this falsification analysis yielded the following results:

| Kill Condition | Status | Key Evidence |
|---------------|--------|--------------|
| AI demand <3% of incremental growth | **NOT MET** | IEA projects ~50% of incremental demand; clearly material |
| US electricity growth <2% annually | **PARTIALLY MET** | 2020-2026 CAGR: **1.7%**; forecasts revised down |
| LNG export growth stalls | **PARTIALLY MET** | European demand down 18-20%; US-to-Europe exports fell 19% |
| EV adoption plateaus | **DEFINITIVELY MET** | First YoY sales decline in 2025 (-2%); current impact only 0.25% of grid |

The evidence reveals that while AI data center demand is genuinely material, the electrification pillar is dramatically weaker than thesis proponents assume, and the LNG export story faces structural European demand destruction that US producers cannot fully offset through Asian market redirection.

---

## Actual electricity demand growth falls short of thesis requirements

The thesis requires electricity demand acceleration from **0.5% historical baseline to 2.5-5% annually**. Actual data contradicts this:

**EIA-reported demand growth:**
- 2023: **-1.3%** (demand actually declined)
- 2024: **2-3%** (weather-inflated; temperature-adjusted: **2.4%**)
- 2020-2026 projected CAGR: **1.7%** (including COVID rebound effects)

Critically, **EIA forecasts have been revised downward, not upward**. The December 2025 Short-Term Energy Outlook cut 2026 demand projections by 1.3 percentage points. [ERCOT's forecast was slashed by 40%](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/energy-short-term-outlook-2026-load-demand-data-centers/807530/)—from 15.7% to 9.6% growth for 2026—because actual large-load demand materialization has disappointed.

According to [Ember Energy's analysis](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/us-electricity-2025-special-report/2024-in-review/), the 128 TWh demand increase in 2024 included approximately **25 TWh attributable solely to hotter weather**—meaning structural demand growth was closer to 2.4% adjusted, barely above the 2% kill threshold and well below the 2.5-5% thesis claim.

## Utility executives call data center demand "overstated by 3-5x"

Perhaps the most damaging evidence against the demand acceleration thesis comes from the industry itself. Major utility CEOs have publicly challenged the AI demand narrative:

**Constellation Energy CEO Joe Dominguez** stated in May 2025: "I just have to tell you, folks, I think the load is being overstated. We need to pump the brakes here." **Vistra CEO James Burke** suggested data center demand projections could be [overstated by 3-5x](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/17/ai-data-center-openai-gas-nuclear-renewable-utility.html) in some jurisdictions as developers shop identical projects across multiple utilities. Former FERC Chairman Willie Phillips acknowledged: "There is a question about whether or not all of the projections, if they're real."

When **AEP Ohio** implemented stricter rules requiring data center developers to commit to 85% of requested capacity regardless of utilization, interconnection requests [dropped from 30 GW to 13 GW](https://epsa.org/getting-the-forecast-right/)—a **57% reduction**. This suggests more than half of projected data center demand may be speculative.

A [Southern Environmental Law Center-commissioned analysis](https://www.selc.org/news/overhyped-data-center-growth-is-shaping-our-energy-future/) found utilities in Southeast states are planning for data center demand with only **0.2% probability of occurring**. The report identified "systemic overstatement" and bias toward overestimation in utility forecasting.

## The electrification pillar is collapsing, not accelerating

The ESG electrification thesis is failing across multiple metrics. **2025 marked the first year-over-year decline in US EV sales** since the market's inception, with unit sales falling 2% from 1.30 million to 1.28 million according to [Cox Automotive](https://www.coxautoinc.com/insights-hub/q4-2025-ev-sales-report-commentary/). Q4 2025 was catastrophic: EV sales plunged **46% quarter-over-quarter** and 36% year-over-year to just 234,000 units—the lowest since Q4 2022.

Current EV electricity demand is **11 TWh annually—just 0.25% of total US consumption**. Even aggressive 2030 projections show EVs adding only 100-300 TWh, representing 2-7% of projected total demand. [Ember Energy confirms](https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/us-electricity-2025-special-report/2024-in-review/) that only 3% of 2024's demand increase (4 TWh of 128 TWh) was attributable to light-duty electric vehicles.

Heat pump adoption, the other major electrification vector, is also stalling. According to [UC Berkeley Energy Institute analysis](https://energyathaas.wordpress.com/2024/04/29/why-are-heat-pump-sales-decreasing/), heat pump shipments declined **12-17% in 2023-2024** despite $2,000 federal tax credits. Low natural gas prices (below $2/MMBtu) and high electricity rates are making electrification economically unattractive.

Major automakers have dramatically scaled back EV investments:
- **Ford**: $19.5B write-down, canceled electric SUV, delayed electric truck 18 months
- **GM**: Abandoned 400K EV target, delayed Orion plant, dropped 1M capacity goal
- **Mercedes-Benz**: Pushed all-EV deadline from 2030 to 2035
- **Volvo**: Extended all-EV timeline by five years

## European LNG demand destruction is structural, not cyclical

The third pillar—LNG exports—faces headwinds from permanent European demand reduction. According to [IEEFA analysis](https://ieefa.org/resources/eu-gas-imports-fall-25-2030-demand-reduction-target-exceeded-once-again), the EU curbed gas consumption **15.6%** from April 2024 to March 2025 versus the reference period. Total EU gas consumption is **down 23% since 2021**.

The [Bruegel European Gas Demand Tracker](https://www.bruegel.org/dataset/european-natural-gas-demand-tracker) shows European demand **18% below** the 2019-2021 average in both 2023 and 2024, with industrial demand "consistently depressed" and showing no signs of recovery. The [IEA's Gas 2025 report](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/102725-oecd-european-gas-demand-forecast-to-fall-up-to-10-from-2024-2030-iea) projects OECD European gas demand will contract an additional **8-10% from 2024 to 2030**, with gas-to-power demand dropping 25% by 2030.

US LNG exports to Europe already **decreased 19% (1.5 Bcf/d) in 2024** versus 2023, with exports to EU/UK specifically down 24%. Meanwhile, **Qatar is expanding from 77 mtpa to 142 mtpa by 2030** at the world's lowest cost—representing a major competitive threat that could capture incremental global demand growth and pressure US export margins.

The counterargument—that US LNG hit a record 111 million metric tons in 2025—is valid, but growth is being driven by Asian market redirection, not sustainable European demand. This creates vulnerability if Asian demand softens or Qatar captures market share.

## Historical precedent suggests efficiency will moderate demand growth

Stanford research fellow and data center energy expert **Jonathan Koomey** argues forecasters are "just assuming NVIDIA's business plan is our growth forecast." His [Latitude Media interview](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/catalyst-a-skeptics-take-on-ai-electricity-load-growth/) highlights critical historical precedent: from 2010-2018, global data center electricity use remained **essentially flat** despite a 6x increase in compute output. Computing efficiency improvements entirely offset demand growth.

This pattern could repeat. Chip efficiency is improving rapidly: **NVIDIA Blackwell** processors are 25x more efficient than CPUs for AI workloads; **AMD** exceeded its 30x25 efficiency goal with 38x improvement. [Epoch AI research](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ml-hardware-energy-efficiency) found leading ML hardware becomes **40% more energy-efficient annually**. Google reported AI query energy costs fell **93% in 2024 alone**.

The [IEA's High Efficiency scenario](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai) shows efficiency improvements could unlock "energy savings of more than 15%," potentially offsetting most demand growth as occurred in the early 2010s.

## Conclusion: Demand convergence story is overstated

The falsification analysis reveals that the "three pillars converging simultaneously" narrative is significantly exaggerated:

**Kill conditions assessment:**
- **Electrification**: Definitively failing—EV sales declining, heat pumps stalling, automakers retreating
- **LNG exports**: European demand destruction is structural and permanent; US exports redirecting but not accelerating
- **AI data centers**: Genuinely material but likely overstated by 2-5x based on speculative interconnection requests
- **Total demand growth**: Tracking **1.7-2.4% annually**, not 2.5-5%—falling short of thesis requirements

The thesis that US natural gas prices will structurally re-rate due to 5x electricity demand acceleration faces substantial contrary evidence. While demand growth has accelerated from the ~0.5% historical baseline, it is not approaching the 2.5-5% projections embedded in bullish natural gas forecasts. EIA forecasts are being revised down, utility executives are publicly calling projections "overstated," and the electrification pillar is weakening rather than strengthening.

**Strongest falsification points:**
1. Actual 2020-2026 electricity demand CAGR of 1.7% (not 2.5-5%)
2. First-ever YoY decline in US EV sales in 2025
3. Utility CEOs stating demand is "overstated by 3-5x"
4. AEP Ohio interconnection requests dropping 57% when commitment required
5. European gas demand down 23% since 2021 with further 8-10% decline projected
6. Historical precedent of 2010-2018 flat data center energy despite 6x compute growth

The demand acceleration thesis is not definitively killed, but it faces more headwinds than the bullish consensus acknowledges. Investors pricing in 5x demand acceleration should recalibrate expectations toward the 1.7-2.5% range that actual data supports.
