# Evidence Resolution: Natural Gas Trifecta Demand Shock

## Research Findings

### Unknown 2: Are All Three Demand Pillars Actually Converging? (Falsification)

**Research Date**: 2026-01-22
**Objective**: Find evidence that this thesis is WRONG

**Kill Condition Assessment**:
> - AI data center electricity demand is <3% of incremental demand growth (noise, not signal)
> - US electricity demand growth remains <2% annually through 2025-2026 (trifecta not materializing)
> - LNG export growth stalls due to European demand destruction or alternative supply (Qatar, Australia)
> - EV adoption plateaus and industrial electrification slows

**Status**: PARTIALLY TRIGGERED

**Key Findings**:

1. **Actual electricity demand growth falls short of thesis requirements**
   - Source: EIA, Ember Energy
   - Credibility: Very High (government data)
   - Content: 2020-2026 CAGR is **1.7%**, not 2.5-5%. 2023 saw -1.3% demand decline. Temperature-adjusted 2024 growth was ~2.4%. EIA forecasts being revised **downward**, not up—December 2025 STEO cut 2026 projections by 1.3 percentage points.
   - Bearing: **Supports kill condition** - demand growth below 2% threshold

2. **Utility executives call data center demand "overstated by 3-5x"**
   - Source: CNBC, Utility Dive (direct executive quotes)
   - Credibility: High
   - Content: Constellation CEO Joe Dominguez: "I think the load is being overstated." Vistra CEO: projections "overstated by 3-5x." AEP Ohio interconnection requests dropped **57%** when commitment required.
   - Bearing: **Supports kill condition** - suggests AI pillar is weaker than claimed

3. **EV adoption has definitively plateaued**
   - Source: Cox Automotive
   - Credibility: High
   - Content: **First-ever YoY decline in US EV sales in 2025** (-2%). Q4 2025 sales plunged 46% QoQ. EVs currently just 0.25% of grid demand (11 TWh). Heat pump shipments down 12-17%.
   - Bearing: **Kill condition MET** - electrification pillar collapsing

4. **European LNG demand destruction is structural**
   - Source: IEEFA, Bruegel, IEA
   - Credibility: Very High
   - Content: EU gas consumption **down 23% since 2021**, 18% below 2019-2021 average. US-to-Europe LNG exports fell 19% in 2024. IEA projects further 8-10% decline through 2030. Qatar expanding from 77 to 142 mtpa by 2030.
   - Bearing: **Partially supports kill condition** - LNG pillar weakening but redirecting to Asia

5. **Historical precedent suggests efficiency will offset demand**
   - Source: Stanford researcher Jonathan Koomey, Epoch AI
   - Credibility: High
   - Content: 2010-2018 global data center electricity use was **flat** despite 6x compute growth. AI hardware efficiency improving 40% annually. Google reported 93% reduction in AI query energy costs in 2024.
   - Bearing: Suggests demand projections may be overstated

**Caveats**: AI data center demand is genuinely material (IEA projects ~50% of incremental demand), but 2-5x overstated by speculative interconnection requests.

### Unknown 1: Supply Response Capacity at $4-5/MMBTU (Validation)

**Research Date**: 2026-01-22
**Objective**: Find evidence that SUPPORTS thesis with clear mechanism

**Conviction Increase Condition**:
> - Basin decline rates are accelerating, requiring more drilling just to maintain production
> - Labor and equipment constraints cap rig count growth at <20% annually
> - Pipeline bottlenecks in Appalachia limit Marcellus takeaway capacity
> - Producer capital discipline holds despite price signals (debt covenants, investor pressure)

**Status**: MET

**Key Findings**:

1. **Basin decline rates accelerating - the treadmill effect**
   - Source: EIA, Enverus, Natural Gas Intelligence
   - Credibility: Very High
   - Content: Haynesville wells drilled in 2022 produced **31% less EUR** than 2019 vintage. First-year decline rates 60-70%. Marcellus requires **900-1,000 new wells annually** just for flat production. DUC inventory at historic lows (~1,615 wells).
   - Mechanism: **Validates thesis** - supply must run faster to stand still

2. **Labor and equipment constraints create hard ceiling**
   - Source: Industry analysis, company filings
   - Credibility: High
   - Content: Workforce at 141,000 vs 200,000+ peak in 2014. Projected **40,000 worker shortage**. Frac spreads at 156 vs 310-320 ceiling. Patterson-UTI retiring 400,000 HP of equipment without replacement. 6-12 month equipment lead times.
   - Mechanism: **Validates thesis** - physical constraints on rapid scaling

3. **Appalachian pipeline bottlenecks persist**
   - Source: EIA, Williams filings
   - Credibility: Very High
   - Content: Marcellus/Utica production 35-37 BCF/day exceeds ~24.5 BCF/day takeaway capacity. Eastern Gas South trades **$0.80/MMBTU discount** to Henry Hub. MVP took **10 years** and cost $7.2B vs $3.5B estimate. Next major additions 2027-2030.
   - Mechanism: **Validates thesis** - cheapest gas stranded from demand centers

4. **2022 price spike proved supply inelasticity**
   - Source: Dallas Fed Energy Survey, producer filings
   - Credibility: Very High
   - Content: At $9+/MMBTU prices, production grew **only 4%**. 59% of executives cited investor pressure for restraint. Pioneer CEO: "Whether $150 oil or $100 oil, we're not going to change our growth plans."
   - Mechanism: **Strongly validates thesis** - revealed preference, not theoretical constraint

5. **Producer capital discipline embedded in 2025-2026 guidance**
   - Source: EQT, Expand Energy, Antero, Range filings
   - Credibility: Very High
   - Content: EQT maintaining $2.00/MMBTU FCF breakeven, targeting debt reduction. Expand guiding 6% growth. Antero running 2 rigs with flat guidance. Only Range targeting meaningful growth (~20% over 3 years).
   - Mechanism: **Validates thesis** - discipline holding despite economics

**Kill Condition Assessment**: NOT TRIGGERED
- Spare capacity: Only 5-10 BCF/day identifiable (not >20 BCF/day)
- Growth mode: Only Range; most flat or <6%
- Rig counts: Structural constraints; 10-20% max realistic
- Pipeline capacity: Clear bottleneck; MVP helped but insufficient

### Unknown 3: Timeline and Capacity for Nuclear Restarts (Analogues)

**Research Date**: 2026-01-22
**Objective**: Find historical precedents with similar dynamics

**Kill Condition**:
> - >5 nuclear restart projects announced with credible 2027-2028 timelines
> - NRC fast-tracks restart approvals (new regulatory posture)
> - Total restart capacity exceeds 8 GW (meaningful vs. ~100 GW existing nuclear fleet)
> - SMR projects achieve commercial operation (not just NRC approval)

**Status**: NOT TRIGGERED

**Key Analogues**:

1. **Watts Bar Unit 2 (2016)** - US completion precedent
   - Predicted: 5 years. Actual: 9 years (1.8x slippage)
   - Cost: $4.7B vs $2.5B projected (88% overrun)
   - Lesson: US nuclear consistently delivers 1.8-2.5x later than promised

2. **Vogtle Units 3&4 (2023-2024)** - New build benchmark
   - Predicted: 4-5 years. Actual: 11-12 years (2.5x slippage)
   - Cost: $36.8B vs $14.3B (157% overrun)
   - Lesson: Even recent projects with massive resources miss dramatically

3. **Japan post-Fukushima (2011-2026)** - Most relevant analogue
   - 54 reactors shut down; only **14-15 restarted in 13 years** (~25% restored)
   - Industry forecasts consistently 50-100% too optimistic
   - Lesson: Even with strong political will and $27B investment, restarts take years
