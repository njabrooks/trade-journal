# Stage 1: Signal Triage

## Selected Claim

### Claim 3: Natural Gas Demand Will Experience 10x Growth Shock from Trifecta

**Level**: main
**Type**: thesis_candidate
**Category**: macro
**Tickers**: UNG, LNG
**Time Horizon**: medium_term
**Qualifier**: high

**Claim**:
US electricity demand is shifting from 15 years of flat 0.5% growth to 2.5-5% annual growth, representing a potential 10x increase, driven by three simultaneous mega-trends: ESG-mandated electrification, AI data center buildout, and LNG export capacity expansion.

**Evidence**:
- Electricity demand flatlined at 0.5% growth from 2005-2020 (Transcript 23:33)
- Latest forecasts: 2.5% to 5% electricity demand growth over next decade (Transcript 24:00)
- JP Morgan's Michael Cembalest "Electrovision" paper documents these trends (Transcript 22:49)
- Equivalent to adding 15 New York Cities worth of electricity demand by 2030 (Transcript 25:14)

**Reasoning**:
Each of the three mega-trends (electrification, AI, LNG exports) is a formidable tailwind independently. Having all three converge simultaneously creates unprecedented demand pull on natural gas as the dominant base load source.

**Backing**:
EIA data shows LNG export demand pull is significantly higher than AI demand pull (Transcript 1:01:22). Even if one pillar fails, remaining two provide substantial support. Historical: electricity demand has never declined, even during "flat" period maintained 0.5% growth.

**Rebuttal**:
- AI bubble burst could remove one pillar quickly (Transcript 50:43)
- Nuclear restarts and SMRs could reduce natural gas share
- Efficiency gains could moderate demand growth projections
- 10-20 year view: potential for energy overbuild and supply glut (Transcript 47:26)

**Supporting Evidence Claims**: claim-21, claim-22, claim-23, claim-24
**Rebutting Evidence Claims**: claim-25

## Supporting Evidence Summary

### Claim 21: Electricity Demand Was Flat 0.5%/Year for 15 Years (2005-2020)
- US electricity demand grew at ~0.5% per year from 2005 to 2020
- Establishes baseline against which current acceleration is dramatic
- Source: JP Morgan Michael Cembalest "Electrovision" paper
- **Qualifier**: high (verifiable historical data)

### Claim 22: Latest Forecasts Project 2.5-5% Annual Electricity Demand Growth
- Energy forecasters revised projections to 2.5-5% annually over next decade
- Doesn't fully count "mad rush towards AI data centers" in late 2025
- Equivalent to adding 15 New York Cities of demand by 2030
- **Qualifier**: medium (forecasts inherently uncertain)

### Claim 23: LNG Export Demand Pull Exceeds AI Demand Pull
- EIA data shows LNG export demand pull is significantly higher than AI
- LNG export capacity doubling from 16-18 BCF to ~30 BCF by end of decade
- **Qualifier**: high (EIA data verifiable)

### Claim 24: Europe Building LNG Regasification After Russia-Ukraine Shock
- Post-2022, Europe building LNG regasification to receive US exports
- Security-motivated demand (not price-sensitive)
- **Qualifier**: high (observable policy shift)

## Rebutting Evidence Summary

### Claim 25: AI Demand Pillar Most Time-Sensitive and Could Come Undone Fastest
- Of three pillars, AI data center buildout is most vulnerable
- Hyperscalers could "turn off the capital spigot" tomorrow
- Thesis works "because you need all three to fail at the same time"
- **Qualifier**: medium (acknowledged risk)

4. **Acknowledged rebuttals**: AI pillar fragility noted, adds credibility
