Crypto is NOT a Fintech Wrapper - Rebuilding the Thesis of Blockchains and Crypto

The frustration in the crypto-native community has never been so intense: with the abrupt change of market environment, many crypto-natives witness  the complete stop on web3 innovation, with dozens of stablecoin projects heading towards the web2 fintech direction, stripping off most innovative features of blockchains - atomic composability, verifiability, trustlessness, and permissionlessness.

While some crypto folks share a pretty pessimistic view: crypto is dead, I have been in a deep reflection on our blockchain industry, and ask myself - what constraints do blockchains and crypto dapps actually collapse, and what potential opportunities can they unlock? Or alternatively, are blockchains and crypto dapps merely fintech wrapper?

Like all new technologies, blockchains arrived wearing old clothes. Early narratives were all rearview-mirror metaphors: “digital cash,” “decentralized PayPal,” “smart contracts = digital legal contracts.” Some of those metaphors were useful, but most were incomplete: I would argue that blockchains are one of collapsers of constraints in our era. Not because they are “databases, but slower,” but because they change who can agree with whom, without first agreeing on who to trust.

To rebuild the thesis of blockchain and cryptos, instead of simply sharing my subjective views, I will try to distill the fundamentals of blockchains and answer the questions of what it does enable, what new opportunities it can unlock, and what values it can create for us and society, etc.

### Individuals: from accounts to keys

- Accounts historically = institution promises; key possession enables ownership/transfer without permission.
- Self-custody introduces operational risk; custodial UX often reintroduces trusted third parties.
- Verifiability shifts “truth” from institutions to observation; reduces customer-support dependency.
- Programmable receipts enable DeFi primitives: atomic exchange, escrow, composability.
- Permissionless reading → permissionless competition → less lock-in.
- Mass-market value: faster/cheaper/more reliable money movement (stablecoins, 24/7 settlement) but requires better risk abstraction.

### Organizations: from central databases to adversarial coordination

- Enterprises already run ledgers; blockchains coordinate with adversarial counterparties.
- BFT lineage; Bitcoin’s permissionless consensus via crypto+incentives.
- Incentive failures exist (selfish mining). Governance matters.
- Ethereum: organization chart can include code.
- PoS changes assumptions about what makes history expensive to rewrite.
- Shared ledger reduces reconciliation, audit overhead.
- Credible neutrality enables rivals to build on same substrate (contestability, composability).
- DeFi as infrastructure: modular stack, ship by composition.

### Economies: from local trust networks to global settlement fabric

- Settlement is core; borders/intermediaries constrain.
- Public verifiable history enables new coordination.
- Scaling/throughput/privacy constraints; succinct verification (SNARKs) suggests settlement+verification layers.
- Infrastructure substrate akin to TCP/IP for value; compounding innovation.
- Unbundling institutions into layers (custody, payments, liquidity, credit, compliance, distribution).
- 24/7 markets create different behaviors and expectations.

### Beyond “tokenizing old processes”

- True breakthroughs require redesign around new constraint profile: native auditability, interoperability, verification over reconciliation.
