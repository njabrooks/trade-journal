Price Discovery and Trading

Kevin describes using a classroom trading simulator to teach price discovery: each student has *perfect but incomplete* private info; through trading, the market aggregates info into the correct price.

### Core teaching case

- Stock GTT will be acquired by one of 5 bidders with bids: $20, $22, $24, $26, $28.
- Over a 10-minute session, each trader receives 3 pieces of always-correct private info: “bidder X dropped out” (eliminates one possible final price).
- Students often default to expected-value logic (EV) and can still lose if they ignore what *others* might know.

### Key lesson

Shift from:
- “What do *I* think it’s worth?”

to:
- “What does the *market* say it’s worth—and what information might be on the other side of my trade?”

Example:
- Trader with outcomes ($22, $28) sees price at $23 and buys (EV=$25) → can lose if final is $22.
- Another trader with outcomes ($20, $22) sees price at $23 and sells aggressively (max possible is $22) → wins.

### Aggregation intuition

If Trader A has ($22,$26) and Trader B has ($26,$28):
- A buys below $26 and sells above $22
- B buys below $28 and sells above $26
→ both express curves, market clears around $26 even if neither “knows” $26 is correct.

### Practical trading reflex

Experienced participants think:
- who is on the other side?
- what do they know?
- why are they trading with me?

Rather than only:
- what do I know?

### Alpha framing

“Low P/E / high FCF yield” isn’t an edge if everyone knows it. Alpha needs information or insight the market doesn’t have, and you must ask:
- what do I know that the market doesn’t?
- why doesn’t the market know it?

### Flow vs information

Markets often can’t distinguish informed vs uninformed flow at the same size (unlike some quote-driven betting markets), so the tape can be noisy.
