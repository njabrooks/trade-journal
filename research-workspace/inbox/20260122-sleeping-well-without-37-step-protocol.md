But… why would you ever regularly deprive yourself of sleep just for a few extra hours of consciousness? It doesn’t make sense. All you’re doing is degrading the quality of those very hours you’re living.

You’ll achieve more than you could possibly fathom. You’ll wake up with a larger reservoir of energy. You’ll be able to exert yourself both physically and mentally at levels your potential deserves.

This post is not going to dive into every little thing about sleep and prescribe you a 37 step bedtime routine. That’s the antithesis and what we want to avoid. We’re going to talk about the essentials. Things anyone could do tonight to improve the quality of your sleep, forever.

What sleep experts and biohackers fail to realize is that most people have lives. Very real lives. The longer your sleep routine, the easier it’ll be to fall off and completely forget about it. It’s the same with diets, and you know my stance on that.

## Sleep Duration & Quality

Quality can matter more than duration. Consider 90-minute cycles; waking from light sleep feels better. Consistent sleep and wake times help set circadian rhythm (melatonin at night, cortisol in morning).

## Your Sleep Environment

Light matters: blue/white light after sunset suppresses melatonin. Suggestions:
- Blue light blocking glasses (orange/red block 90–99%)
- Night shift / red-screen accessibility on phone
- Prefer more small warm lights vs one bright white light
- Blackout blinds / sleep mask; earplugs
- Cool room ~64ºF (18ºC)

## Stimulants

Alcohol harms deep sleep. Avoid caffeine after ~3pm (rule of thumb).

## Nasal Strips

Nasal strips can help nasal breathing; place ABOVE nostrils. Consider deviated septum check; mouth tape can help habitual mouth breathing.

## Move Your Body

Daily physical exertion improves sleep quality.

## Supplements

Keep it simple:
- Glycine (earlier in evening; can intensify dreams)
- Magnesium glycinate orally + magnesium chloride topically
- Stretching before bed

Random tips:
- Use a “safety alarm” later than needed to allow natural waking
- Get outside at sunrise/sunset
- Warm drinks ~1.5h before bed

Melatonin: avoid constant use; low dose ok for jetlag/timezone shifts.
