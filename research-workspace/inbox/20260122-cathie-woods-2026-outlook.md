# Cathie Wood’s 2026 Outlook: The US Economy Is A Coiled Spring

**Author:** Cathie Wood  
**Published:** January 15, 2026  
**Reading time:** ~35 min

## Full text (extracted)

Happy New Year to ARK’s investors and other supporters! We are very grateful for your support.

As I illustrate in this letter, we do believe that investors have lots of reasons for optimism! I hope that you enjoy our discussion. In the context of economic history, we are at an important moment.

### COILED SPRING

Despite sustained real gross domestic product (GDP) growth during the past three years, the underlying US economy has suffered a rolling recession and has evolved into a coiled spring that could bounce back powerfully during the next few years. In response to COVID-related supply shocks, the record-breaking 22-fold surge in the Fed funds rate from 0.25% in March 2022 to 5.5% in the sixteen months ended July 2023 pushed housing, manufacturing, non-AI capital spending, and low-to-middle income America into recession, as illustrated in the charts below.

As measured by existing home sales, housing dropped 40% from 5.9 million at an annual rate in January 2021 to 3.5 million in October 2023, a level last seen in November 2010 and around which it has fluctuated for the past two years. Illustrating how tightly the spring has coiled, existing home sales are as low as they were in the early 1980s when the US population was ~35% lower than today.

As measured by the US PMI (Purchasing Managers Index), manufacturing has been contracting for roughly three years. According to this diffusion index, 50 is the demarcation point between expansion and contraction.

Meanwhile, capital spending, as measured by non-defense capital goods excluding aircraft, peaked in mid-2022, a level to which it has just returned both with and without technology. Indeed, since its blowoff in the tech and telecom bubble, this measure of capital spending had struggled for more than 20 years to break out, until 2021 when COVID-related supply shocks forced a step-up in both digital and physical investment. What once was the cap in spending seems to have become a floor now that the AI, robotics, energy storage, blockchain technology, and multiomics sequencing platforms are ready for prime time. After the tech and telecom bubble of the nineties, a 20-year peaking process around $70 billion has given way to what could become the most powerful capital spending cycle in history, as illustrated below. In our view, an AI bubble is years away!

Meanwhile, as measured by the University of Michigan, the sentiment of low- and middle-income earners has not been this low since the early eighties when double-digit inflation and interest rates ravaged purchasing power and pushed the US economy into back-to-back recessions. Moreover, in recent months, high income sentiment has capitulated as well.

### Deregulation Alongside Lower Taxes, Inflation, And Interest Rates

Thanks to the confluence of deregulation and lower taxes (including tariffs), inflation, and interest rates, the rolling recession which has characterized the last few years in the US could turn quickly and sharply during the next year and beyond.

Deregulation is unleashing innovation in every sector, led by the first AI and Crypto Czar, David Sacks, in the AI and digital assets space. Meanwhile, lower taxes on tips, overtime, and social security should hand US consumers significant refunds this quarter, potentially driving real disposable income growth up from ~2% at an annual rate during the second half of 2025 to ~8.3% this quarter.

In addition, corporate tax refunds should surge, as accelerated depreciation on manufacturing facilities, equipment, software, and domestic research and development (R&D) pushes the effective corporate tax rate down toward 10%.

Inflation, as measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), is likely to drop to a surprisingly low—if not negative—rate during the next few years, driven by declines in oil prices, home prices, and other factors.

### Productivity Boom

If ARK’s research on technologically enabled disruptive innovation is correct, non-farm productivity growth should accelerate to 4-6% on a year-over-year basis for both cyclical and secular reasons during the next few years, driving unit labor cost inflation even lower.

Convergences among AI, robotics, energy storage, public blockchain technology, and multiomics technology are likely to push productivity growth to sustainable new highs and generate significant wealth creation.

In the short term, technologically enabled productivity gains could continue to slow employment growth in the US, pushing unemployment to 5.0%+ and encouraging the Fed to continue cutting rates.

AI training costs are declining 75% per year, and AI inference costs are dropping up to 99% per year.

ARK would not be surprised to see nominal US GDP growth hover in the 6-8% range during the next few years, thanks to 5-7% productivity growth, 1% labor force growth, and -2% to +1% inflation.

#### The Dollar’s Outlook

ARK notes a narrative of declining US exceptionalism and discusses conditions under which the dollar might strengthen (comparison to Reaganomics).

#### AI Hype

ARK notes that 2025 data-center systems investment grew 47% to nearly $500bn, expected to reach ~$600bn in 2026. ARK argues returns may accrue to AI-natives and that consumer adoption of AI is extremely fast.

ARK notes equity valuations are high, but argues notable bull markets can occur while P/E multiples compress, especially amid productivity-driven real GDP acceleration and inflation deceleration.
