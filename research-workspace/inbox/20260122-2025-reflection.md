2025

Naturally, as a systematic global macro investor leaving 2025, I reflected on the mechanics of what happened, particularly in the markets. That's what today’s reflection is about.

Though the facts and returns are indisputable, I see things differently from most others. While most people see US stocks and particularly US AI stocks to be the best investments and hence the biggest investment story of 2025, it is indisputably true that the biggest returns (and hence the biggest story) came from 1) what happened to the value of money (most importantly the dollar, other fiat currencies, and gold) and 2) US stocks significantly underperforming both non-US stock markets and gold (which was the best performing major market) principally as a result of fiscal and monetary stimulations, productivity gains, and big shifts in asset allocations away from US markets.

Regarding 1) what happened to the value of money: the dollar fell by 0.3% against the yen, 4% against the renminbi, 12% against the euro, 13% against the Swiss franc, and 39% against gold (which is the second largest reserve currency and the only major non-fiat currency). So, all fiat currencies fell, and the biggest story and the biggest market moves of the year were the result of the weakest fiat currencies falling the most, while the strongest/hardest currencies strengthened the most.

The best major investment of the year was long gold (returning 65% in dollar terms), which outperformed the S&P (18% in dollars) by 47%. Or said differently, the S&P fell by 28% in gold-money terms.

Key principles:
- When one's currency goes down, it makes it look like things measured in it went up (weak denominator effect). S&P +18% USD equals -28% in gold terms.
- Currency moves shift wealth, buying power, inflation, and trade flows with a lag. Always be hedged to your least-risk currency mix.
- Bonds promise money: when money value falls, real worth falls even if nominal prices rise. 10y Treasuries +9% USD equals -34% in gold terms.

Dalio argues debt assets are unappealing (especially long duration) as large debt needs rolling and the Fed likely eases; yield curve steepening probable.

Regarding 2) US stocks underperforming: in strong currencies, US stocks looked weaker; they underperformed other equity markets. European stocks outperformed US by 23%, China by 21%, UK by 19%, Japan by 10%. EM stocks returned 34%.

US stock gains in 2025 came from earnings growth (+12%), P/E expansion (+5%), and dividends (~1%). Magnificent 7 earnings +22%, rest of S&P +9%.

Valuations look stretched (high PEs, tight spreads). Dalio’s long-term US equity expected return ~4.7% (sub-10th percentile) vs bond returns ~4.9% → low equity risk premium. If rates rise due to supply/demand pressures as money declines, could hurt credit and stocks.

He expects Fed bias to push nominal and real rates down (supporting prices/bubbles). Productivity likely improves, but distribution between profits vs wages/taxes is uncertain (right/left politics).

Illiquid markets (VC/PE/real estate) still have problems; liquidity premiums likely rise.

Politics/geopolitics: shift from multilateralism to unilateralism, higher conflict risk and military spending; sanctions/protectionism/deglobalisation; more gold demand; reduced foreign demand for US debt/dollars.

Recommends understanding historical patterns, cause/effect relationships, systemized game plan, AI + data.
